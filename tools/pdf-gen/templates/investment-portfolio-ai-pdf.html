How to use these prompts

Eight decisions every self-directed investor hits across a fiscal year. Eight prompts you can copy, paste, and adapt — designed for the free tier of your favourite AI assistant.

Each prompt pairs with a specific tab in your Investment Portfolio Tracker. You'll see the tab name on each page — paste, fill in placeholders, send.

How it works
  1. Open the prompt page you need (Allocation Advisor, Tax-Loss Harvesting Scout, etc.).
  2. Open the matching tab in your spreadsheet.
  3. Copy the prompt into your favourite AI assistant.
  4. Replace the [PLACEHOLDERS] with your data.
  5. Read the worked example on the same page to see what good output looks like.
What you'll need
  • An account with any AI assistant (the free tier works)
  • Your Investment Portfolio Tracker open in another window
  • 15 minutes the first time; ~3-5 minutes once familiar
What this PDF won't do

It won't execute trades. It won't move money between accounts. It won't file your taxes. It won't rebalance your 401k allocation. You paste, you read, you decide. The AI proposes the move; you execute it at your broker, plan administrator, or accountant's office.

Your account numbers, brokerage logins, SSN, and routing numbers never enter any AI tool. Use the spreadsheet's labels ("Brokerage Taxable," "401k Plan") instead of identifiers. Ticker symbols are public information — safe to share freely. Everything happens in your own AI account.

The 8 prompts assume you've already entered your positions. If you haven't, fill out the Holdings Master tab first; everything downstream depends on it.

Turn the page when you're ready.

1. Allocation Advisor — Drift from target. What to rebalance.

Pairs with: 📈 Asset Allocation
The prompt
You are diagnosing my asset allocation drift and recommending specific rebalancing trades. Be precise about contribution-based vs sell-based rebalancing given my tax situation. MY CURRENT ALLOCATION (paste from Asset Allocation tab): [Asset class | Current $ | Current % | Target % | Drift] MY CONTEXT: - Age: [number] - Years to FIRE / retirement target: [number] - Last rebalance date: [date] - Tax-loss harvesting opportunities currently available (yes/no): [yes/no] - Unrealized gains in taxable accounts: $[amount] - New monthly contributions available for redirection: $[amount/month] - Constraints: [401k mid-quarter rebalancing locked? other constraints?] For each asset class: 1. ABSOLUTE DRIFT — current % minus target % 2. STATUS — On target (within ±2pp) / Mild drift (2-5pp) / Significant drift (>5pp) 3. CAUSE — price appreciation, depreciation, neglected contributions, new contribution Then: 1. RANK the drifts by significance + tax-cost-to-correct 2. RECOMMEND 1-3 specific rebalancing moves with $ amounts: - Contribution-rebalancing (redirect new contributions) — preferred when tax cost would be high - Sell-rebalancing (sell appreciated to buy underweight) — only when tax cost is low or in a tax-advantaged account 3. FLAG any move that has tax implications + estimated tax cost 4. EXPLAIN the trade-off if suggesting selling appreciated taxable holdings Don't recommend leveraged ETFs. Don't recommend "diversify into alts." Don't recommend financial advisor services.
Worked example — output
Example (TECHCO senior SWE · age 38 · $280K · 12 yrs to FIRE)

Allocation: US Eq 56% / Intl 11% / Bonds 8% / REITs 5% / Metals 5% / Crypto 6% / Cash 5% / RSU 2%. Target: 50/15/12/5/5/3/5/5. Drifters: US +6.4pp, Intl -3.6pp, Bonds -4.1pp, Crypto +3.4pp.

Output:

Move 1 (THIS MONTH): Redirect 100% of $1,800/mo contributions to Bonds (via 401k + Roth IRA). Bond gap closes in 6.4 months. Zero tax cost.

Move 2 (MONTH 7+): Once bonds gap closes, redirect to International. Gap closes in 5.6 months. Zero tax cost.

Move 3 (DEFER): US Equities overweight stays. Don't sell appreciated taxable shares to rebalance — you'd realize ~$3,600 in LTCG for a correction natural contribution-rebalancing handles in 12 months.

One thing NOT to do: Don't rebalance via leveraged inverse-equity ETFs (SQQQ, SH) to "hedge" the US overweight. Those decay due to volatility drag — they compound LOSSES over multi-month holding.

2. Tax-Loss Harvesting Scout — Wash-sale-safe sell/buy pairs

Pairs with: 📉 Tax-Loss Harvesting
The prompt
You are scouting tax-loss harvesting opportunities. Identify positions with unrealized losses, name wash-sale-safe replacement securities, estimate tax savings, and flag any 30-day wash-sale-window risk. MY POSITIONS WITH UNREALIZED LOSSES (paste from Tax-Loss Harvesting tab): [Ticker | Account | Shares | Cost basis avg | Current price | Holding period | Unrealized $ loss | % loss] RECENT TRADING ACTIVITY (last 30 days + planned next 30 days): - Bought in last 30 days: [list with ticker + date] - Sold in last 30 days: [list] - Planned buys next 30 days: [list] MY TAX CONTEXT: - Marginal federal rate: [%] - State rate: [%] - Estimated short-term capital gains this year: $[amount] - Estimated long-term capital gains this year: $[amount] - Carry-forward losses from prior years: $[amount] For each candidate position: 1. RECOMMENDED ACTION — Harvest now / Wait / Skip (and why) 2. WASH-SALE WINDOW CHECK — any same-or-substantially-identical security bought 30 days before OR planned 30 days after (in ANY account including IRA, including spouse's accounts) 3. WASH-SALE-SAFE REPLACEMENT — name 1-2 alternatives that maintain similar exposure without triggering wash-sale 4. ESTIMATED TAX SAVINGS — short-term at marginal rate; long-term at LTCG rate; include state Then: 1. RANK harvesting candidates by tax savings × execution risk 2. FLAG any position where holding-period day-count matters 3. ONE THING NOT TO DO this cycle CRITICAL: Wash-sale rule applies across ALL your accounts including spouse's accounts and IRAs. Don't suggest a sell that creates a wash-sale via a recent IRA buy. Be paranoid about this.
Worked example — output

BND (-$450 long-term): 🟡 Wait — opportunity exists but small. Wash window clear. Replace with AGG or SCHZ. ~$85 saved — skip unless harvesting concurrently.

VXUS (-$414 long-term): 🔴 SKIP — wash-sale risk. You bought 5 shares VXUS in Roth IRA 8 days ago. IRS would disallow $414 loss AND IRA basis is NOT adjusted (permanent tax loss). Wait 22 more days, OR sell to non-substantially-identical international ETF NOW.

ARKK (-$648 short-term): 🟢 HARVEST NOW. Wash window clear. Replace with QQQ / XLK / FBCG. $200 tax saved (short-term loss offsets ordinary income at marginal rate). Best single harvest opportunity this cycle.

One thing NOT to do: Don't harvest VTI (your largest position) just because it's down 2% this month. The $400/mo auto-contribution + recent 12-day-ago purchase would trigger wash-sale — disrupting contribution flow isn't worth the harvest.

Total potential harvest: ~$328 this cycle (ARKK + VXUS after window).

3. Concentration Risk Alerter — Single position, sector, employer

Pairs with: 🎁 Options & RSUs + 📊 Holdings Master
The prompt
You are auditing my portfolio for concentration risk across three dimensions: single-position risk / sector risk / employer-stock risk. Name specific positions where I'm dangerously exposed and tell me what to do. MY HOLDINGS (paste from Holdings Master): [Ticker | Account | Shares | Current price | Position $ | % of portfolio] EMPLOYER STOCK / RSUs / ESPP (paste from Options & RSUs): [Grant type | Grant date | Vest schedule | Vested $ in portfolio | Unvested $ remaining] MY CONTEXT: - Employer industry: [tech / healthcare / finance / consumer / etc.] - Years at current employer: [number] - Other income sources (primary income only / secondary income / etc.): [pattern] - Equity-grant cadence: [annual refresh / one-time / sporadic] - Lockup periods or sale restrictions currently active: [yes/no] THRESHOLDS to flag against: - SINGLE POSITION >20% = HIGH risk - SECTOR >40% = HIGH risk (industries are correlated) - EMPLOYER STOCK >10% = HIGH risk (compounds employment risk) For my portfolio: 1. CALCULATE: total % in top-1 / top-3 / top-5 positions 2. CALCULATE: % per GICS sector (Tech / Healthcare / Financials / etc.) 3. CALCULATE: % in employer-related securities (vested RSUs + ESPP + employer stock) 4. FLAG every threshold-breaking concentration For each flagged concentration: 1. WHAT'S THE RISK in plain English (use historical examples — Enron 2001, Lehman 2008) 2. THE TWO MOVES that reduce concentration 3. EMPLOYER-STOCK SPECIFIC: sell-on-vest vs hold for LTCG 4. ONE NON-OBVIOUS RISK (e.g., your tech ETF + employer tech RSUs together = sector + employer compounding)
Worked example — output

Top-1 (VTI): 30.6%. Top-3: 51%. Top-5: 73.7%.

🟡 VTI 30.6% — MILD (index ETF, not single-stock risk). Don't sell to "diversify within US equities" — you'd buy something similar. Reduce by NOT contributing more to VTI; let drift normalize.

🔴 TECH SECTOR ~32% — HIGH RISK. Your direct tech (AAPL/MSFT/GOOGL) + tech-heavy ETF weighting + your EMPLOYER is tech + your salary is tech = single-sector dependency. Lehman 2008 = financial workers with bank stock + bank job hit doubly. Move: Stop adding to AAPL/MSFT/GOOGL; overweight defensive (XLV / XLP) at $200/mo.

🟢 EMPLOYER STOCK 3.1% — within band. BUT unvested $39K over next 3 yrs could push to 15-18% if you don't sell-on-vest. Default policy: sell on vest. The 1-yr LTCG wait costs more than the LTCG-vs-STCG differential — extra 6-15% volatility tax on TECHCO underperformance.

Non-obvious risk: Tech sector ~32% + tech RSUs + tech job = three things move together in a 2000-style cycle. Hedge = secondary income + non-tech sector overweight + 6-mo cash buffer.

4. Look-Through Analyzer — Your true exposure under the hood

Pairs with: 🗂️ ETFs & Mutual Funds
The prompt
You are analyzing the underlying holdings of my ETFs and mutual funds to reveal my TRUE portfolio exposure. Look-through reveals hidden concentration that ticker-level analysis misses. MY FUND HOLDINGS (paste from ETFs & Mutual Funds tab): [Ticker | Position $ | % of portfolio | Fund objective] FOR EACH FUND, PASTE THE TOP-10 HOLDINGS from the fund's official disclosure: - VTI top 10: [paste list with weight %] - VOO top 10: [paste] - VXUS top 10: [paste] - (etc.) MY INDIVIDUAL STOCK HOLDINGS (separate from funds): [Ticker | Position $ | % of portfolio] For my portfolio, calculate: 1. TRUE EXPOSURE PER MEGA-CAP STOCK (combining direct + ETF look-through): - For each of top 10 names across all funds: total $ exposure + % of portfolio - Compare to my "direct" position (what I see in Holdings Master) 2. HIDDEN OVERLAPS — funds whose top-10 holdings significantly overlap (paying ER on duplicates) 3. HIDDEN CONCENTRATIONS — single stocks exceeding 5% via look-through (even though they don't appear that big at ticker level) Then: 1. THE TOP 3 LOOK-THROUGH CONCENTRATIONS — combined % of portfolio 2. THE LARGEST FUND OVERLAP — which two funds share the most 3. ONE RECOMMENDATION to reduce hidden concentration 4. ONE INSIGHT about my portfolio I probably didn't realize
Worked example — output

Top 3 look-through concentrations:

1. AAPL: 7.9% of portfolio (5.2% direct + 2.7% via VTI/VOO look-through). You THINK 5.2%; you actually have 7.9%.

2. MSFT: 6.9% (4.4% direct + 2.5% look-through).

3. GOOGL: 5.2% (3.7% direct + 1.5% look-through).

Largest fund overlap: VTI and VOO are 80-90% identical in top-10 (S&P 500 is included in VTI). Combined $112K = 40% of portfolio is functionally ONE large US equity position split across two ETFs. You're paying expense ratios on both for essentially the same exposure.

Recommendation: Don't accumulate both — pick one (VTI for broader coverage including mid/small caps). Let VOO drift down as % of portfolio.

Insight you probably didn't realize: The "30.6% in VTI" you see is actually ~7.9% in AAPL alone — mega-cap names grew so large in cap-weighted indices that two of your ETFs share heavy weighting in the same handful of stocks. This isn't a problem IF "be heavy in mega-cap US tech" is your thesis. If you bought VTI + VOO thinking you were diversified, you were buying the same companies twice.

5. Market Scenario Analyst — Drop 30%? Add $5K/mo? Impact + recovery.

Pairs with: 🎯 Scenario Simulator
The prompt
You are running scenario analysis on my portfolio. Two types: market shocks (drop X%, rally Y%) and contribution changes (add/remove $/month). For each, calculate impact AND recovery timeline. MY PORTFOLIO (paste from Holdings Master + Asset Allocation): - Total portfolio value: $[amount] - Asset class breakdown: [%] - Average annual return assumption (historical real): [%] - Annual contribution rate: $[amount/yr] SCENARIOS TO RUN: MARKET SHOCKS: - Mild correction: -10% equities, -5% bonds, -25% crypto - Moderate: -20% equities, -10% bonds, -50% crypto - Severe: -35% equities, -15% bonds, -75% crypto (2008-style) - Mild rally: +15% equities, +5% bonds, +50% crypto - Strong rally: +30% equities, +10% bonds, +100% crypto CONTRIBUTION CHANGES: - Add $500/mo / Add $1,000/mo / Reduce $500/mo / Pause 12 months MY FIRE TARGET: - FIRE number: $[amount] - Years to FIRE (current trajectory): [#] For each scenario: 1. POST-SHOCK PORTFOLIO VALUE 2. % CHANGE from current 3. RECOVERY TIMELINE given continuing contributions 4. PSYCHOLOGICAL CHECKPOINT — worst-case interim valuation 5. RECOMMENDED ACTION during this scenario (often: do nothing; sometimes: rebalance) End with: SCENARIO MOST LIKELY in next 10 yrs / SCENARIO MOST WORTH PREPARING FOR / ONE WRONG REACTION per scenario. Don't predict. Run the math. The math doesn't predict — it conditions.
Worked example — output

Moderate correction (-20% equities): Portfolio $215K (-23%, -$65K). Recovery ~18 months. FIRE delay ~12 mo. Action: Continue contributions. Don't try to time the bottom. The -25 to -30% drawdown phase is where investors capitulate; investors who sell here lock in losses.

Severe (-35%): $158K (-44%). Recovery ~36 mo. FIRE delay ~24 mo. This is the BEST contribution environment of the cycle. If job secure, INCREASE contributions. Lump-sum cash buffer if you have one. 2008-2009 actually dropped 50%+ before recovery; second-leg-down trap is where most retail capitulates.

Add $500/mo extra: FIRE 12yr → 10yr 7mo. Saves 1yr 5mo. Single highest-leverage action you control.

Most likely: Mild correction (-10 to -15%) every 2-3 years. Normal market behavior — budget for it.

Most worth preparing for: Severe correction (-35 to -50%) once per decade. Not "likely" in any given year (~10-15%) but likely SOMEWHERE in your 12-yr FIRE window. Preparation = emergency fund + clear "don't sell during drawdown" rule written down BEFORE the drawdown.

Wrong reactions: Mild → sell to "preserve capital" (locks in 11% loss). Moderate → stop contributing (misses buying opportunity). Severe → capitulation at -40 to -50% (biggest wealth-destroyer in retail investing).

6. Dividend Income Optimizer — Yield-boosting moves, no timing risk

Pairs with: 💰 Dividend Income Calendar
The prompt
You are optimizing my dividend income WITHOUT chasing yield. Specifically: improving yield-on-cost without excessive risk, smoothing cash-flow calendar across the year, identifying tax-inefficient placements. MY DIVIDEND POSITIONS (paste from Dividend Income Calendar tab): [Ticker | Shares | Account | Annual div $ | Yield-on-cost % | Yield-current % | Frequency | Ex-div months] MY CONTEXT: - Total dividend income last 12 months: $[amount] - % of portfolio generating dividends: [%] - Marginal federal tax rate: [%] - State rate: [%] - Dividend reinvestment policy: [reinvest all / cash out / per-position] - Income need: [retirement supplement / current spending / pure reinvest] - Years to needing dividend income: [#] For my dividend positions: 1. CALENDAR ANALYSIS — month-by-month projected for next 12 months. Flag cash-gap months. 2. TAX-PLACEMENT — flag high-dividend payers misplaced in taxable. REITs pay non-qualified — these belong in IRAs. 3. YIELD-CHASE RISK — flag positions >7% yield (often distressed / yield traps). 4. CASH-GAP-FILLING — 1 ticker to add that pays cash-gap months. 5. DRIP vs CASH-OUT per position based on years-to-income-need. End with: THREE MOVES / ONE TICKER TO RESEARCH / ONE THING NOT TO DO. Don't recommend specific high-yield stocks just for yield. Don't recommend MLPs/BDCs without explaining tax complexity. Verify qualified vs non-qualified status.
Worked example — output

Cash-gap months: Jan/Apr/Jul/Oct = $0. Quarterly cycle (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) is heavy.

🔴 VNQ IN TAXABLE = MISPLACED. Non-qualified REIT dividends taxed at 24% federal + 6.85% state = 30.85%. If VNQ were in Roth IRA, $170/yr tax-free forever. Current annual drag: $52/yr. Move 1: Swap VNQ to Roth IRA at next contribution cycle.

Move 2: Stop adding to SCHD/VYM (similar). Pick DGRO instead — dividend-growth focus matters more than current yield over 12-yr horizon. Today's 3% grower becomes tomorrow's 5%+ yield-on-cost.

Move 3: Avoid BDCs / MLPs / mREITs to "fill cash gaps." 8-12% yields with structural complexity (Schedule K-1 filings, UBTI risk in IRAs, mREIT unsustainable yields).

One ticker to research: O (Realty Income) — monthly-paying retail REIT. Hold in Roth (non-qualified). Research before buying.

One thing NOT to do: Don't buy QYLD or covered-call income ETFs marketed as 10-12% yield. The "yield" is mostly return-of-capital (your own money) — NAV erodes; total-return-negative for buy-and-hold. Yield trap.

7. Position Health Check — Fundamentals + risk summary

Pairs with: 📊 Holdings Master + 📈 Stocks Tracker
The prompt
You are evaluating the health of a specific position I own. Quick diagnostic — not deep research, but enough to decide hold / trim / sell / add. THE POSITION: - Ticker: [SYMBOL] - Shares: [#] - Cost basis avg: $[per share] - Current price: $[per share] - Position size: $[total] / [% of my portfolio] - Holding period: [years/months] - Account: [taxable / IRA / 401k / Roth] - My original thesis when I bought: [why I bought, what I expected] CONTEXT FOR EVALUATION: - Current market cap: $[approx] - Sector: [GICS] - Recent news / events (last 90 days that I know of): [list] - Dividend status: [pays / doesn't pay / cut / suspended] PROVIDE QUICK DIAGNOSTIC: 1. FUNDAMENTAL CHECK — current P/E, P/S, FCF yield, debt/equity vs sector median 2. THESIS STATUS — is the original reason still true? Has the company executed? 3. RISK CHECK — sector-specific risks (commodity / regulatory / customer concentration) 4. POSITION-SIZE CHECK — over-concentrated relative to conviction? Under-allocated relative to thesis? 5. TAX-LOT STATUS — short-term vs long-term + unrealized gain/loss Then: 1. RECOMMENDED ACTION — hold / trim / sell / add (with reasoning) 2. ONE METRIC TO MONITOR going forward (the one number that would change your conclusion) 3. ONE RED FLAG that would trigger immediate sell 4. ONE WAY TO IMPROVE THESIS UNDERSTANDING before any major action Don't recommend specific dollar targets. Don't predict price. Be specific about which metric to watch, not where it should be.
Worked example — output

AAPL diagnostic: P/E ~29x (sector median 24x; mature large-cap 18x). FCF yield ~3.5% (healthy). Net cash positive. Nothing extreme in either direction.

Thesis check: ✅ Premium brand intact. ✅ Services growth confirmed (~13% YoY, now ~22% of revenue). 🟡 AI integration lagging vs MSFT/GOOGL — not yet a thesis breaker but worth monitoring.

Risk: 🔴 China geopolitical exposure ~17-19% of revenue + manufacturing concentration. Largest risk. 🟡 App Store regulatory ~$25B/yr at risk in worst-case unbundling.

Position-size: 5.2% direct + 2.7% look-through = 7.9% true. Upper edge of "reasonable" for high-conviction single-name.

Recommended action: HOLD, but stop accumulating direct AAPL. Redirect new contributions away; let position drift as % of portfolio.

One metric to monitor: Services revenue growth rate. If services growth slows below 8% YoY for two consecutive quarters, bull case weakens — hardware-only AAPL trades at 18-22x P/E historically.

One red flag → sell: Major US-China decoupling event (tariffs expanding to consumer goods) disrupting manufacturing. Tactical trim 50% on such a headline.

8. Quarterly Portfolio Review

Pairs with: 📊 Annual Summary
The prompt
You are writing my quarter-end portfolio review. Look at the full quarter and tell me what worked, what didn't, and what to do next quarter. QUARTER DATA (paste from Annual Summary / Performance & Returns): - Portfolio start of quarter: $[amount] - Portfolio end of quarter: $[amount] - Quarter change: $[amount] ($/%) - Year-to-date change: $[amount] ($/%) - Best month / Worst month: [month + change %] CONTRIBUTIONS + DRAWS THIS QUARTER: - Total contributed: $[amount] - Total withdrawn: $[amount] - Major one-offs (bonus / RSU vest): [list] PERFORMANCE BREAKDOWN: - Best class / Worst class / Best position / Worst position DRIFT + REBALANCING: - Largest drift / Rebalancing moves made / Dividends received vs BENCHMARK: - Portfolio quarter return: [%] - S&P 500 quarter return: [%] - Custom benchmark return: [%] - Outperformance: [pp] Give me: 1. THREE WINS — specific outcomes better than planned 2. THREE MISSES — honest diagnosis, not pep talk 3. THREE LESSONS — patterns I now see (not "rebalance more" — observations about HOW I'm investing) 4. THREE PRIORITIES NEXT QUARTER — specific actions with $ targets 5. ONE STRUCTURAL CHANGE to consider (account move, allocation shift, tax-treatment opt) Each section under 80 words. No motivational ending. CFO writes a board update — that's the tone.
Worked example — output

Three wins: (1) RSU vest $13K preserved as portfolio capital, not lifestyle spending. (2) Tech sector overweight worked — +21.4% AAPL while broad market rallied. (3) International underperformance contained — only -2.1% on a quarter the dollar rallied.

Three misses: (1) NO REBALANCING despite Q2 ending +4pp overweight US equities; Q3's tech rally pushed drift to +6.4pp. (2) RSU $13K wasn't redirected to underweight bonds/international — treated as "windfall stays invested" rather than "windfall fills allocation gap." (3) 401k bond/international contribution flow was zero — auto-contributions still equity-heavy default.

Three lessons: (1) Drift detection without rebalancing trigger = drift accumulates. Need hard-coded rule: >5pp = rebalance regardless of opinion. (2) RSU vests are tax-FREE rebalance opportunity (sell-on-vest = no incremental gain). Use them. (3) 401k allocation needs manual quarterly review — defaults are equity-heavy by design.

Three priorities Q4: Sell vested RSU $6K → buy bonds/intl. Redirect 100% of $1,800/mo to bonds + intl for 6 mo. Audit 401k fund allocation.

Structural change: Set CALENDAR-BASED REBALANCE RULE: end-of-Q4 every year, rebalance to target within ±2pp regardless of opinion. Calendar > opinion.

Which AI should I use?

All eight prompts work in free tiers. Differences come down to output style + how each handles long position data.

Conversational, follow-up editing

Best for: Market Scenario Analyst, Quarterly Portfolio Review, Position Health Check, Dividend Income Optimizer. Most AI assistants have a conversational tone, write narrative summaries cleanly, and are fluent at "explain this differently" iteration.

Limit: Free tiers cap how much you can paste per message (~3,000-4,000 words). If you paste 20+ positions with multiple ETF top-10 holdings, you'll exceed context. Batch by category.

Longer structured input

Best for: Allocation Advisor, Tax-Loss Harvesting Scout, Concentration Risk Alerter, Look-Through Analyzer. Some assistants are better at multi-position tables, structured calculations, wash-sale-window checks that need precise rule application, and handle longer portfolio data in one paste.

Limit: Free tiers often cap conversations per day. If you hit it, switch to another AI assistant for the next prompt.

A paid AI plan

Worth it if running quarterly reviews + monthly TLH scans across 30+ positions. A paid plan can ingest your full Holdings Master + 5-fund top-10 disclosures in one paste. For ≤20 positions, free works.

Universal tips
  • Paste as TEXT, not screenshots. Free-tier AI doesn't read spreadsheet screenshots well.
  • Never paste account numbers, brokerage logins, SSN, routing numbers. Use labels ("Brokerage Taxable," "Roth IRA") instead. Ticker symbols + approximate position sizes are public-information-equivalent and safe to share.
  • Wash-sale rule applies across ALL accounts including spouse's accounts and IRAs. Tax-Loss Harvesting Scout: include EVERY account's recent activity — missing one IRA buy creates a wash-sale you'll discover at tax time.
  • Position Health Check is FAST but SHALLOW. Don't treat AI output as substitute for reading the 10-K + earnings transcript. Use AI to surface questions; do the reading to answer.
  • Save good output. Paste sharp Quarterly Reviews + concentration analyses into the matching tab's Notes column. Build your own playbook over quarters.

Your portfolio data never leaves your AI conversation. The AI never sees your spreadsheet — only what you paste, only during that chat. Your brokerage credentials never enter the picture.
