How to use these prompts

Seven questions every debt payoff journey runs into. Seven prompts you can copy, paste, and adapt — designed for the free tier of your favourite AI assistant.

These aren't generic. Each prompt is built to pair with a specific tab in your Debt Payoff Planner. You'll see the tab name on each page — paste the prompt, fill in the placeholders from that tab, send.

How it works
  1. Open the prompt page you need (Payoff Strategy Optimizer, Settlement Letter Generator, etc.).
  2. Open the matching tab in your spreadsheet.
  3. Copy the prompt into your favourite AI assistant.
  4. Replace the [PLACEHOLDERS] with your data from that tab.
  5. Read the worked example on the same page to know what good output looks like.
What you'll need
  • An account with any AI assistant (the free tier works)
  • Your Debt Payoff Planner open in another window
  • 10 minutes the first time; ~3 minutes once you're used to the flow
What this PDF won't do

It won't run prompts for you. You paste. You read. You decide. The AI suggests; you choose. That separation is on purpose — your debt, your call.

Your credit-bureau credentials, account numbers, and SSN never enter any AI tool. The prompts only ever see what you paste — usually balances, APRs, and minimums, not account numbers. None of it sends back to us; everything happens in your own AI account.

1. Payoff Strategy Optimizer
📊Pairs with: Strategy Comparison
The Prompt
You are helping me pick the right debt-payoff strategy. I have a mix of debts and need to know whether to use Snowball (smallest balance first), Avalanche (highest APR first), or a Custom order. Be specific. Quantify the trade-off in dollars + months. MY DEBTS (paste from Debt List tab): [PASTE TABLE: Debt Name · Type · Balance · APR · Minimum · Due Day] MONTHLY BUDGET AVAILABLE FOR DEBT: $[total monthly across all debts] MY PSYCHOLOGY: [pick one — "I need quick wins to stay motivated" / "I want maximum math optimization" / "Mixed — emotional priorities matter too"] INCOME: $[net monthly income] EXTRA AVAILABLE / MONTH (above minimums): $[amount] Give me four things: 1. THE NUMBER for each method — months to debt-free, total interest paid, and the dollar delta between Snowball and Avalanche for MY specific data. Don't generalize; calculate. 2. THE FIRST DEBT to attack under each method, with the exact monthly amount (own minimum + any extra). Tell me which debt I'll see paid off first and roughly when. 3. A RECOMMENDATION ranked by fit to my psychology — including a "Hybrid Custom" order if there's a smarter sequence than pure Snowball or pure Avalanche. 4. ONE COUNTERINTUITIVE OBSERVATION about my debt mix that I'd miss if I just ran the numbers (e.g., a promo period ending soon, an unusually high APR worth refinancing first, a small balance that's emotionally outsized). Stay specific. Cite debt names + dollar amounts from my data.
Worked Example
Example Input (excerpt)

Chase Sapphire · Credit Card · $4,250 · 21.99% · $85 · 5th

Amex Gold · Credit Card · $2,890 · 26.99% · $58 · 22nd

SoFi Personal · Personal Loan · $8,400 · 11.49% · $220 · 1st

Subaru Auto · Car Loan · $12,500 · 5.99% · $340 · 15th

Sallie Mae · Student Loan · $18,900 · 4.89% · $198 · 10th

Care Credit · Medical · $840 · 0.00% promo · $35 · 27th

Total minimums: $936. Extra $250/mo available. Income: $5,800. Psychology: "Mixed".

Example Output
The Numbers (your specific data):

Avalanche: 47 months · $5,840 total interest · first target Amex Gold (26.99%)

Snowball: 49 months · $7,120 total interest · first target Care Credit ($840 smallest)

Delta: Avalanche saves $1,280 and 2 months for your specific data.

Recommendation (your psychology = mixed):

Hybrid: Kill Care Credit FIRST anyway — its 0% promo ends in ~12 months and APR will jump. Then switch to Avalanche on Amex Gold. You get one quick emotional win + maximum math after.

Counterintuitive Observation:

Your Amex Gold is only $2,890 — small enough to feel surmountable AND highest APR. Pure Snowball + Pure Avalanche both pick differently; the Hybrid path lets you target it second after Care Credit and still feel the win.

MY CURRENT STATE (paste from Credit Score Tracker + Simulator): Current FICO score (avg of bureaus): [score] Payment history (% on-time, last 12 mo): [%] Total CC utilization: [% — total balance ÷ total limit] Per-card utilization (highest %): [card name + %] Average age of accounts: [years] Hard inquiries (last 24 mo): [count] Recent derogatory marks: [list any late payments / collections / charge-offs in last 2 years] Open accounts: [number] Credit mix: [cards / loans / etc.] GOAL: hit [target] score by [date] Give me five things: 1. THE GAP ANALYSIS — for each FICO factor (Payment History 35%, Utilization 30%, Length 15%, Mix 10%, New 10%), how strong is my current sub-score and what's the realistic ceiling? 2. THE TOP 3 ACTIONS ranked by point gain, with estimates (e.g., "Drop card X utilization from 78% to 28% by 6/15 → +25 points"). Be conservative; under-promise. 3. THE TIMING — for each action, when in my billing cycle should it land? (Statement-close date matters more than payment-due date for utilization gains.) 4. WHAT NOT TO DO over the next 90 days — closing old cards, opening new ones, large purchases that spike utilization. 5. A REALISTIC CEILING — given my current trajectory, what's the highest score I can actually hit in 90 days? In 1 year? Don't promise 800 if I'm at 670 with active derogatory marks. Stay specific. Cite my card names and percentages.
Worked Example
Example Input

Current FICO 692 (avg). Payment history 100% on-time (last 12mo). Total util 47%. Highest per-card: Amex Gold 78%. Avg age 4.3 yr. Hard inquiries 4 (last 24mo). No derogatory marks. 5 cards, 1 student loan. Goal: 740+ by year-end.

Example Output
Gap Analysis:

Payment History (35%): Strong. At ceiling already.

Utilization (30%): Weak at 47%. Biggest lever — getting to <10% would add ~50 pts.

Length (15%): Moderate. 4.3 yrs is fine; don't close old cards.

Mix (10%): OK. 5 cards + 1 loan is healthy.

New Credit (10%): Mildly hurt by 4 inquiries — they'll age off over next 24mo.

Top 3 Actions (90-day window):
1. Drop Amex Gold to ≤10% by 6/15 → +30–40 pts. Pay $2,300 to bring balance from $4,680 to $600. Make payment by 6/12 (4 days before statement close on 6/16).
2. Drop Chase Sapphire to ≤10% by 7/20 → +10–15 pts. Currently 38% util. Pay $1,400 before 7/17 statement close.
3. Don't open any new cards or take any new loans until 10/1 → preserves +5–10 pts. Every new inquiry costs 5–10 pts immediately.
Realistic Ceiling:

90 days: 730–745 likely. 1 year: 760–780 if you maintain <10% util and add no new credit. Hitting 800+ requires 24+ months of clean payment history at <10% util — possible but not by year-end.

3. Utilization Timing Advisor
💡Pairs with: Utilization Optimizer
The Prompt
You are timing my credit card payments for maximum score impact. Credit bureaus report based on the balance at STATEMENT CLOSE, not payment-due date. Tell me exactly what to pay and when, per card. MY CREDIT CARDS (paste from Utilization Optimizer tab): [PASTE TABLE: Card · Balance · Limit · Util % · Statement Close Day · Due Day] GOAL: get total CC utilization to ≤[%] by [target date] EXTRA AVAILABLE THIS MONTH: $[amount] For each card with utilization above target, give me: 1. THE EXACT $ TO PAY — the amount that brings reported balance to the target % on the next statement close. 2. THE DEADLINE — date 2–3 days before statement close (banks need processing time). Be specific: "Pay $X by [date], not on [date]." 3. THE ORDER — if my extra $ is limited, which card to prioritize first for max point gain. Generally that's the highest individual-card utilization, since FICO scores both per-card AND overall utilization. 4. THE NON-OBVIOUS CARD-LEVEL TRAP — e.g., a card that's reporting on a non-obvious cycle, an authorized-user account that's dragging me down, an old card that should stay open even though I never use it. 5. WHAT WILL THE SCORE DO — a rough 30-day projection in points after the next reporting cycle, given the payments above. Conservative estimate. Stay specific. Use my card names, my limits, my close dates.
Worked Example
Example Input

Chase Sapphire: $1,240 bal / $10,000 limit / 12% util / close 6th / due 28th

Amex Gold: $4,680 bal / $6,000 limit / 78% util / close 16th / due 8th

Citi Double Cash: $340 bal / $5,000 limit / 7% util / close 12th / due 4th

Discover It: $0 / $4,500 limit / 0% util / close 22nd / due 14th

Goal: total util ≤10% by 7/15. Extra available this month: $2,500.

Example Output
Per-card plan:

Amex Gold (78% util): Pay $4,080 by 6/13 (3 days before 6/16 statement close). Brings balance to $600 → reports as 10%. Score impact alone: +30–40 pts.

Chase Sapphire (12% util): Already under target. Pay $240 by 7/3 to drop to 10% next cycle.

Citi (7%) + Discover (0%): Leave alone.

Priority order with $2,500 budget:

1. Amex: $2,500 against the $4,080 needed → drops Amex to ~36% util. Still big improvement.

2. Next month: hit the remaining $1,580 to get Amex below 10%.

Non-obvious trap:

Amex Gold is a charge card historically — it reports utilization differently than revolving cards. Confirm with Amex which behavior your specific card uses (Charge Card has no preset limit; Pay Over Time has one).

30-day projection:

If you pay $2,500 to Amex by 6/13: +15–25 pts after 7/15 reporting. Hit the full $4,080 by 7/13 and you'd see +30–40 pts.

4. Consolidation Intelligence
🔄Pairs with: Debt Consolidation
The Prompt
You are picking the single best consolidation path for my debt. I have multiple high-APR debts and I'm considering Personal Loan, Balance Transfer card, or HELOC. Tell me which path saves the most money AND has the lowest risk for MY situation. MY DEBTS (paste from Debt List): [PASTE TABLE: Debt · Balance · APR · Min Payment] THREE OPTIONS I'M EVALUATING: A. Personal Loan: $[total balance], [APR]% fixed, [months] term, origination fee [%] B. Balance Transfer: $[transfer cap], 0% promo [months], [%] transfer fee, then [%] regular APR C. HELOC: $[available], [APR]% variable, [years] draw period, [years] repayment period, fees $[amount] MY CONTEXT: Credit score: [current] Income: $[monthly net] Home equity (if applicable): $[amount] Risk tolerance: [low/medium/high] Discipline level: [honest assessment — will I rack up new debt on cleared cards?] For each option, give me: 1. TOTAL INTEREST PAID over the life of the consolidation vs status quo. 2. THE BREAK-EVEN POINT (when does the consolidation start saving money after fees). 3. THE FAILURE MODE — what specific behavior would make this option backfire? (Personal Loan: maxing the cards back up. BT: not paying off before promo ends. HELOC: putting the house at risk.) 4. THE RECOMMENDATION — which option fits my discipline + risk profile, and why? Be willing to recommend AGAINST consolidation if status quo + Avalanche is better for me. Don't give me 3 mediocre options. Pick ONE and defend it.
Worked Example
Example Input

Debts: Chase $4,250 / 21.99%, Amex $2,890 / 26.99%, Care Credit $840 / 0% promo, SoFi loan $8,400 / 11.49%. Total: $16,380. Avg minimums $398/mo.

Options: A. Personal Loan $16k @ 10% / 60mo / 3% fee. B. BT $7,140 @ 0% for 18mo / 4% fee. C. HELOC $50k @ 6.99% variable / 10yr.

Context: 720 FICO, $5,800/mo income, owns home with $80k equity. Risk: medium. Discipline: "I've maxed cards out twice before."

Example Output
By the numbers:

A. Personal Loan: Total interest $4,420 + $480 fee = $4,900. Break-even: 14 mo. Saves $1,640 vs status quo.

B. Balance Transfer (cards only, $7,140): Net interest $0 + $286 fee = $286. SoFi loan untouched. Break-even: month 1. Saves $2,180 IF paid off in 18 months.

C. HELOC: Total interest $5,720 + $400 fees = $6,120. Break-even: 17 mo. Saves $420 vs status quo. Puts the house at risk.

Failure modes:

Your specific risk: You said "maxed cards twice before." That eliminates Option B for you — you'd clear Amex + Chase, then run them back up to ~$3,000 each by month 12. End state: original debt + new BT card debt = worse than status quo.

Recommendation:

Option A — Personal Loan $16k @ 10% / 60mo. Saves $1,640 + forces a closed-loan structure that you can't accidentally re-borrow against. Pay off Care Credit's 0% promo with cash flow first. Then take the loan. Then close 2 of your 4 cards (keep oldest for credit-mix). HELOC is off the table — your discipline self-rating + variable rate + house collateral = no.

SPEED RANK — fastest to first dollar (e.g., DoorDash, Instacart, TaskRabbit). 2. SKILL RANK — best use of one of my listed skills (e.g., freelance writing, tutoring, design contracts). 3. Day job: Mid-level marketing analyst at SaaS company, $68k/yr. Hours available: 8/wk. Skills: SQL, copywriting, Spanish (fluent), spreadsheets. Risk: medium. Need: 60 days. Region: Austin TX.

6. Settlement Letter Generator
📋Pairs with: Debt List
The Prompt
You are drafting a hardship / settlement letter to a creditor. The goal: get them to (a) waive a late fee, (b) reverse a penalty APR, (c) accept partial settlement on a charged-off debt, or (d) reduce the minimum payment temporarily. Be specific. Don't write generic. WHAT I'M ASKING FOR: [pick one — late-fee waiver / APR reset / partial settlement / payment plan / dispute / goodwill removal] CREDITOR: [name] ACCOUNT: [last 4 of account, NOT full number — never put full account in AI] SITUATION: • Account opened: [year] • Original balance: $[amount] • Current balance: $[amount] • Status: [current / 30-day late / 60+ late / charge-off / collection] • Late fee or penalty being contested: $[amount] • Why I'm asking: [job loss / medical / one-time mistake / divorce / etc.] • What I'm offering instead: [amount + terms — e.g., "settle at 40% of balance, paid in 3 installments"] MY LEVERAGE: • Customer for [X] years • Otherwise on-time history: [%] • Other accounts with this bank: [list] Write me: 1. THE OPENING — name + relationship + ask in one sentence. 2. THE SITUATION — 2-3 sentences. Factual, not pleading. 3. THE ASK — exactly what you want. Specific amount, specific terms, specific deadline. 4. THE OFFER — what you'll give them in return (continued business, lump-sum, payment-plan commitment). 5. THE CLOSE — clear next step + your contact info placeholder. Tone: professional. Don't beg. Don't apologize excessively. Banks have legal departments — assume your letter will be scanned, not read. Lead with the ask. Make the action obvious.
Worked Example
Example Input

Ask: Late-fee waiver + APR reset to original.

Creditor: Chase Sapphire Preferred (acct *4382)

Situation: Opened 2019. Original $0 (revolving). Current $4,250. Status: current now, was 5 days late on April payment. $35 late fee + penalty APR 29.99% triggered (was 21.99%).

Why: Autopay failed because checking account was closed at job change. Single missed payment in 5 years.

Leverage: 5 years, 99.7% on-time, 3 other Chase accounts (checking, savings, mortgage).

Offering: Continued Chase loyalty + commitment to autopay setup within 7 days.

I'd appreciate a written response within 14 days. I can be reached at [your email] or via secure message in the Chase app.

Thank you for considering.

[Your name] / [account 4382]

Which AI should I use?

All seven prompts work in free tiers. The differences are output style, input length, and how well the AI handles structured-data pastes.

Conversational, follow-up editing

Best for: Payoff Strategy Optimizer, Income Acceleration Coach, Settlement Letter Generator. Most AI assistants have a conversational tone and are great at writing the hardship-letter drafts and revising creditor scripts ("more apologetic," "more direct," "less detail about the hardship").

Limit: Free tiers cap how much you can paste per message (~3,000–4,000 words). If your Debt List is 15+ rows, paste in two batches — debts 1-10 then 11-20 — and ask the AI to combine.

Longer structured input

Best for: AI Credit Score Coach, Utilization Timing Advisor, Consolidation Intelligence, Debt Health Score Coach. Some assistants handle longer structured input (multiple cards with balances + APRs + limits) more cleanly and produce table-formatted, math-anchored output.

Limit: Free tiers often cap conversations per day. If you hit a wall, switch to another AI assistant for the next prompt.

A paid AI plan

Worth it if you're actively running the plan monthly. The Settlement Letter and Consolidation prompts especially benefit from the longer context window so you can paste your full Debt List + the lender's first response in one shot.

Universal tips
  1. Paste as text, not screenshots. Free-tier AI doesn't read spreadsheet screenshots reliably.
  2. Never paste account numbers, full SSN, or full card numbers. Use placeholders ("Chase Sapphire — $5,200 balance" not "Card ending 1234567890").
  3. Iterate. First response is rarely the keeper. "Make it shorter" / "be more specific" / "rerun assuming the lender already declined once" — the AI revises on request.
  4. Save what works. Found a creditor script that got a fee waived? Paste it into the Debt List's Notes column so it doesn't disappear with the conversation.

Your debt data never leaves your AI conversation. Your AI assistant doesn't see your spreadsheet — only what you paste, and only during that chat. No bureau, no Plaid, no aggregator.
