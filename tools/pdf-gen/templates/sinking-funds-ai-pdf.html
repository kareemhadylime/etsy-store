How to use these prompts

Seven decisions every sinking-fund saver hits. Seven prompts you can copy, paste, and adapt — designed for ChatGPT's free tier or Claude's free tier.

Each prompt is built to pair with a specific tab in your Sinking Funds Planner. You'll see the tab name on each page — paste, fill in placeholders, send.

How it works
  1. Open the prompt page you need (Reallocation, Vehicle Advisor, etc.).
  2. Open the matching tab in your spreadsheet.
  3. Copy the prompt into ChatGPT or Claude.
  4. Replace the [PLACEHOLDERS] with your data.
  5. Read the worked example on the same page to see what good output looks like.
What you'll need
  • A ChatGPT account (free tier works) OR a Claude account (free tier works)
  • Your Sinking Funds Planner open in another window
  • 10 minutes the first time; ~3 minutes once familiar
What this PDF won't do

It won't buy the gold, transfer the CD, or rebalance your ETFs. You paste, you read, you decide. The AI proposes the move; you execute it in your brokerage or bank.

Your account numbers never enter any AI tool. Use the spreadsheet's fund names ("Christmas fund," "Down Payment fund") instead of account identifiers. Everything happens in your own AI account.

Turn the page when you're ready.

1. Reallocation
🎯Pairs with: Priority Matrix
The Prompt
You are telling me which sinking fund needs my next dollar this month. Rank by urgency × funding gap. MY ACTIVE FUNDS (paste from Fund Manager): [Fund | Target | Target date | Current balance | Vehicle | Monthly contribution] MY AVAILABLE TO SAVE THIS MONTH: $[amount] NEW MONEY SOURCES (if any): [bonus / tax refund / freelance / etc.] CONSTRAINTS: - Goals I cannot delay: [list — e.g., car insurance due Sept] - Goals I'm willing to slip: [list, if any] For each fund, calculate: - Months remaining until target date - Monthly contribution needed to fully fund - Urgency score (1–10) based on how close to target + consequence of miss - Whether I'm ON TRACK / AT RISK / FALLING BEHIND at current pace Then: 1. RANK funds by where the next dollar should go. 2. RECOMMEND the specific $ amount to each top fund this month. 3. FLAG any fund I should reduce or pause to fund the priority. 4. EXPLAIN the trade-off if there's tension (you delay X to fund Y — what does that cost). Use concrete dates and dollar amounts. Skip "consult a financial planner."
Worked Example
Example Input

Christmas · Target $1,200 · Dec 15 · Balance $200 · Cash · $100/mo

Car repair (annual) · $1,500 · Mar 31 (next year) · $450 · HYSA · $90/mo

Vacation · $3,000 · Jun 30 (next year) · $600 · ETF · $200/mo

Down payment · $40,000 · 5 years · $8,200 · ETF mix · $500/mo

Available: $890/mo total. Bonus $1,800 expected Nov 15.

Example Output
Per-fund analysis (today = mid-August):

Christmas: 4 mo. Need $250/mo. Currently $100. ⚠️ AT RISK.

Car repair: 7.5 mo. Need $140/mo. Currently $90. ⚠️ AT RISK (mild).

Vacation: 10.5 mo. Need $228/mo. Currently $200. ✓ ON TRACK (mild gap).

Down payment: 60 mo. Need $530/mo. Currently $500. ✓ ON TRACK.

Ranked — where next dollar goes:
1. Christmas (+$150/mo): fixed date, can't slip. Total $250/mo through Dec.
2. Car repair (+$50/mo): consequence-of-miss is real. Total $140/mo.
3. Vacation (+$28/mo): small bump to stay on track. Total $228/mo.
4. Down payment (-$28/mo temporarily): slowest target, easiest to defer. Drop to $472/mo through Dec.
This month's totals:

$250 + $140 + $228 + $472 = $1,090 vs your $890 available = $200 short.

Budget reconciliation options:

A. Reduce Down payment by $200/mo Aug–Dec (5 × $200 = $1,000 deferred). Apply Nov 15 bonus to recover.

B. Reduce Vacation by $100 + Down payment by $100. Both still on track.

Trade-off note:

Option A delays home target ~1–2 months ($1,000 × 5% = ~$50 of growth foregone). Worth it to keep Christmas on track.

November bonus play:

$1,800 → $1,000 Down payment (catch up), $400 Christmas (overshoot = buffer), $400 Car repair (fully funds 3 mo ahead).

2. Vehicle Advisor
🪣Pairs with: Fund Manager
The Prompt
You are picking the right savings vehicle for each of my funds. Cash, CD ladder, ETF, money market, or precious metal. Base it on time horizon + risk tolerance + tax situation. MY FUNDS (paste from Fund Manager): [Fund | Target | Target date | Current balance | Current vehicle | Monthly contribution] MY CONTEXT: - Risk tolerance for each fund (some funds I'd never put in stocks): [brief notes] - Tax bracket (rough): [federal % + state %] - Available accounts: [taxable / Roth IRA / HSA / 529 / etc.] - Current rates I have access to: [HYSA APY %, CD rates %, etc.] For each fund, recommend: 1. THE VEHICLE — cash / HYSA / CD / short bond / ETF mix / dividend stocks / precious metal. 2. WHY — based on time horizon (under 1yr → cash/HYSA; 1–3yr → CD or short bond; 3–5yr → ETF mix; 5+yr → growth ETF; metal/crypto only for diversification, not primary). 3. WHERE — Roth IRA / HSA / Taxable / 529 / etc., based on tax efficiency. 4. ONE TRADE-OFF specific to this fund (e.g., "ETF carries 20–30% short-term volatility; if your target is firm, this is risk"). End with TWO FUNDS where I'm in the WRONG vehicle currently + what to switch to + how soon. Don't recommend life insurance or annuities. Don't push paid services.
Worked Example
Example Input

Emergency Fund · $9,000 target · forever · $3,200 · checking · $300/mo

Christmas · $1,200 · Dec 15 · $400 · HYSA · $100/mo

Vacation · $3,000 · Jun 30 next year · $600 · HYSA · $200/mo

New Car · $25,000 · 4 years · $5,500 · HYSA · $400/mo

Retirement supplement · (annual top-up) · $0 · none · $200/mo new

Risk-averse on EF + Christmas. ETF OK for 3+ yr. Tax bracket 22% federal + 5% state. HYSA 4.5%. 3-yr CD 4.8%. Roth IRA $6,500 room. HSA $3,000 room.

Example Output

1. Emergency Fund — HYSA (not checking — you're losing $144/yr at 4.5%). <1yr horizon. Trade-off: T-Bill MMF pays ~0.3% more but adds friction; not worth it for $9K.

2. Christmas — HYSA ✓ already correct. 4-mo horizon = cash only.

3. Vacation — HYSA ✓. 10.5-mo horizon. A 6-mo CD at 4.85% beats 4.5% marginally — only worth it if you can lock $600 and not touch.

4. New Car — ⚠️ WRONG. HYSA too conservative for 4yr. Switch to 60% ETF + 40% short-bond/CD ladder in taxable brokerage. Expected real return 3–7% vs 4.5%. Over 4yr × $25K = ~$2K–3K difference. Glide to cash starting month 36.

5. Retirement supplement — Index ETF in Roth IRA ($6,500 room is exactly what Roth is for). $200/mo × 32yr × 7% real = ~$280K tax-free.

Two wrong-vehicle switches:
1. Emergency Fund: checking → HYSA this week. Earn the extra $144/yr.
2. New Car: HYSA → 60/40 ETF/bond mix in taxable brokerage within the month. Auto-invest $400/mo split appropriately.

3. Metals Coach
🥇Pairs with: Precious Metals Tracker
The Prompt
You are interpreting this month's precious metals price moves for me. Explain what happened, why (briefly), and whether my position needs action. MY HOLDINGS (paste from Precious Metals Tracker): [Metal | Ounces | Cost basis avg | Storage location] THIS MONTH'S DATA: - Gold spot (current): $[amount]/oz vs. last month $[amount]/oz vs. 12-mo average $[amount]/oz - Silver spot (current): $[amount]/oz vs. last month $[amount]/oz vs. 12-mo average $[amount]/oz - Platinum / palladium (if I hold): [same format] MY METALS-IN-PORTFOLIO ALLOCATION TARGET: [%, default 5–10%] MY CURRENT METALS % OF NW: [%] Give me: 1. WHAT HAPPENED — month-over-month price move per metal, plain English (% change + $ value of my position). 2. WHY (BRIEFLY) — 2–3 sentence narrative. Macro driver (rates / dollar / geopolitical / sentiment). Skip the bull/bear essays. 3. POSITION CHECK — am I over, at, or under my allocation target? 4. RECOMMENDATION — do nothing / add / trim / rebalance. Be specific. 5. ONE THING I SHOULDN'T DO this month even though it might be tempting. Skip "consult a precious metals dealer." I'm asking you because I already own this.
Worked Example
Example Input

Holdings: Gold 8 oz @ $1,920 avg cost. Silver 80 oz @ $24.50 avg cost.

This month: Gold $2,640 (vs $2,580 last mo, $2,490 12mo avg). Silver $30.20 (vs $29.50 last mo, $27 12mo avg).

Allocation target: 7%. Current metals % of NW: 9.5%.

Example Output
What happened:

Gold: +2.3% MoM, +6.0% vs 12mo avg. Your 8 oz now $21,120 (vs $20,640) — gained $480.

Silver: +2.4% MoM, +11.9% vs 12mo avg. Your 80 oz now $2,416 (vs $2,360) — gained $56.

Total metals: $23,536. Up $536 this month.

Why (briefly):

Gold continues its slow rally driven by Fed rate-cut expectations + persistent dollar weakness. Silver is following gold higher but also benefiting from industrial demand in semis + solar. No single dramatic catalyst — just continuation.

Position check:

9.5% vs 7% target. Over by 2.5 ppt. Over-allocation is from appreciation (not buying) — both metals rose faster than the rest.

Recommendation:

Trim 1.5–2 oz of gold. Selling 2 oz at $2,640 = $5,280 cash. Redeploy: refill HYSA emergency fund if below target, OR add to broad-market ETF. Don't sell silver — small position; trimming both is unnecessary.

One thing not to do:

Don't buy more gold this month. Already over target. The "metals will keep climbing" story is the same one the 2011 silver-top buyers told themselves. Rebalancing on the way up matters more than on the way down.

4. Dividend Planner
📊Pairs with: Stocks & Dividends
The Prompt
You are looking at my dividend calendar and flagging months where dividend income won't cover the spending I've planned against it. Then recommending action. MY DIVIDEND HOLDINGS (paste from Stocks & Dividends tab): [Ticker | Shares | Payout schedule | Last payout amount | Next ex-dividend date] MY DIVIDEND-FUNDED OBLIGATIONS: - Recurring monthly use of dividends: $[amount] (e.g., HYSA top-up, income supplement) - Planned big-spend items in next 12 months funded by dividends: [list — what, when, $ amount] MY GOAL FOR DIVIDENDS: [reinvest all / pay out all / split — what %?] For each of the next 12 months: 1. ESTIMATED DIVIDEND INCOME (last known per-share × shares for each ticker). 2. PLANNED OBLIGATIONS hitting that month. 3. NET CASH FLOW — surplus or shortfall. Then: 4. FLAG cash-gap months (negative net) with specific reasons. 5. RECOMMEND fixes — DRIP some payouts off / shift big-spend to surplus months / hold cash for a specific month. 6. ONE TICKER you might consider adding to fill a gap month. Quarterly cycles cluster; some tickers pay off-cycle to smooth this. Don't recommend specific stock purchases beyond the "fill the gap" suggestion. I'll evaluate.
Worked Example
Example Input

KO (Coca-Cola) · 200 sh · Quarterly (Apr/Jul/Oct/Jan) · $0.485/sh

VYM (high-div ETF) · 80 sh · Quarterly (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) · $0.92/sh

O (Realty Income, monthly REIT) · 60 sh · Monthly · $0.265/sh

SCHD · 50 sh · Quarterly (Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec) · $0.78/sh

Obligations: $80/mo to HYSA + $500 Dec gifts + $400 Apr vacation deposit + $600 May car insurance. Goal: 50% DRIP / 50% payout.

Example Output
Monthly projection (50% payout):

Jan: KO $97 + O $16 = $113 → $56.50 cash. Obligations $80. ⚠️ -$23.50.

Feb: O $16 → $8. Obligations $80. ⚠️ -$72.

Mar / Jun / Sep / Dec: VYM $74 + SCHD $39 + O $16 = $129 → $64.25. Mar/Jun/Sep -$16. Dec -$516 (gifts).

Apr / Jul / Oct: KO + O = $113 → $56.50. Apr -$424 (vacation). Jul/Oct -$23.50.

May / Aug / Nov: O only → $8. May -$672 (insurance). Aug/Nov -$72.

Pattern:

Hilariously seasonal. Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec higher; Feb/May/Aug/Nov tiny. Obligations mostly flat. Chronic shortfalls.

Fixes:
1. Raise DRIP to 70/30. Your stream isn't enough for $80/mo + big spends. Currently 50% out = ~$507/yr. The $80/mo recurring needs $960/yr. Fund HYSA from paycheck; DRIP more dividends.
2. Shift vacation deposit Apr → Jun. June has $64 cash; April only $56 vs $400 hit.
3. Shift car insurance. May is worst ($8 vs $600). If renewable Apr or Jun, switch. If not, build $600 line-item in Bill Calendar treating May as known gap.
Add-one-ticker suggestion:

Consider MAIN (Main Street Capital, monthly BDC) or another monthly payer. 30–50 shares → $50–90/mo evenly, smoothing Feb/May/Aug/Nov. Just one ticker to evaluate; do your own research.

5. Life Stage Advisor
🤖Pairs with: AI Savings Advisor (hub)
The Prompt
You are detecting my current life stage and suggesting the next sinking fund I should open. Don't recommend funds I already have. Don't recommend generic categories — be specific. MY CURRENT FUNDS (paste from Fund Manager): [list of fund names + targets + balances] MY LIFE CONTEXT: - Age: [number] - Household: [single / partnered / married / kids count + ages] - Career stage: [early / mid / late / transition / business owner] - Housing: [renting / owning + remaining mortgage] - Health considerations (if relevant): [brief] - Big life events expected next 18 months: [list — wedding / new baby / job change / move / parent eldercare / retirement / etc.] For my detected life stage: 1. WHAT FUNDS ARE TYPICAL for someone in my exact stage (be specific — not "save more" but "Emergency Fund 3-month coverage" / "Specific Vehicle Replacement Fund" / "Property Tax Fund"). 2. WHICH ONES I'M MISSING — checking against my current list. 3. THE TOP 3 to open NOW, ranked by priority. 4. FOR EACH RECOMMENDED FUND: target amount + target date + suggested vehicle (paired with Vehicle Advisor logic from page 4). Skip "talk to a financial advisor." Skip insurance products unless directly relevant (e.g., HSA contribution fund makes sense; whole-life insurance doesn't).
Worked Example
Example Input

Current funds: Emergency Fund $4,500/$9,000 · Vacation $1,200/$3,000 · Christmas $400/$1,200 · Down payment $12,000/$40,000.

Age: 33. Married, 1 kid (age 2). Mid-career SWE at public co. Renting in suburb. Health: kid has mild asthma. Expected: considering 2nd kid in 18mo; wife's job may shift to part-time after.

Example Output
Life stage detection:

"Young family + accumulating + planning major transition." Young kid, considering 2nd, dual income trending to 1.5, pre-home, mid-career stability.

Funds typical for your stage:

Emergency Fund (3–6 mo) · Income transition buffer · Childcare reserve · Medical out-of-pocket (HSA) · Vacation · Home down payment · Christmas/gifts · Vehicle replacement · Second-child fund · Aging parent contingency.

Missing (vs your current list):

Income transition buffer · Childcare reserve · Medical/HSA · Vehicle replacement · Second-child fund · Aging parent contingency.

Top 3 to open now:
1. Income Transition Buffer. Target $14,400 ($1,200/mo × 12 mo of wife's expected drop). Date: 18 months. Vehicle: HYSA + Roth IRA contributions. Start $200/mo from Vacation surplus + $200 redirected from Down Payment temporarily.
2. Second Child Fund. Target $8,000 (birth OOP $3K + first-year gear/medical $5K). Date: 18 months. Vehicle: HSA if max-able, else HYSA. Start $400/mo. Pause when not actively trying.
3. Vehicle Replacement Fund. Target $15,000 (used kid-hauler within 5 years). Date: 4 years. Vehicle: ETF + bond mix. Start $150/mo.
Don't open yet:

Aging Parent Contingency — relevant, but defer until tier-1 funds in place. Retirement supplement — belongs in 401k/Roth via paycheck, not sinking funds.

Adjustment:

Pause Down Payment for next 18 months. Income transition is more urgent than home-purchase timeline. Resume after Income Transition is funded. Home purchase delays ~2 years; financial stability through 2nd-kid transition is solid.

6. Annual Fund Review
📊Pairs with: Annual Summary
The Prompt
You are writing my year-end review of sinking funds. Look at the full 12 months across all funds and tell me what worked, what didn't, and what to change next year. ANNUAL DATA (paste from Annual Summary tab): - Total contributed across all funds this year: $[amount] - Total withdrawn (used as planned + emergencies): $[amount] - Funds that hit their target on time: [list] - Funds that missed target (or moved date): [list with notes — why missed] - New funds opened this year: [list] - Funds closed/completed: [list] - Largest single withdrawal: $[amount] for [reason] PER-VEHICLE PERFORMANCE (paste if Pro tier): - Cash/HYSA: total earned interest $[amount] - CDs: maturity gains $[amount] - ETFs: net gain/loss $[amount] - Metals: net gain/loss $[amount] YoY (vs last year if available): - Total saved difference - Funds completed difference Give me: 1. THREE WINS — funds that went better than planned. Specific. 2. THREE MISSES — funds that fell short. Honest diagnosis, not pep talk. 3. THREE LESSONS — structural patterns. Not "save more" but observations about HOW I save. 4. THREE PRIORITIES NEXT YEAR — specific funds with target $ + vehicle + monthly contribution. 5. ONE STRUCTURAL CHANGE to consider (move vehicles, change frequency, automate, etc.). Each section under 80 words. No motivational ending.
Worked Example
Example Input

Contributed: $9,600. Withdrawn: $4,700 ($3,800 planned + $900 furnace emergency).

Hit: Vacation, Christmas. Missed: Down Payment (-$1,200), Vehicle Replacement (-$600, paused).

Opened: Vehicle Replacement (Mar). Largest withdrawal: $2,400 vacation in Aug.

Vehicles: Cash $215 · CDs $340 · ETF +$190 (volatile yr). YoY: +$1,400 saved vs LY; +1 fund completed.

Example Output
Three wins:

1. Christmas hit with $300 to spare — Jan–Mar front-load pattern, second year confirming. 2. Vacation paid in cash for the first time, $200 left over. 3. Furnace $900 absorbed by EF without touching sinking funds — EF is doing its job.

Three misses:

1. Down Payment fell $1,200 short — Sep/Oct extra-deposits stopped while auto-contribution held. 2. Vehicle Replacement paused mid-year, lost momentum by July. 3. ETF allocation underperformed (~$100 of expected gain lost to volatility).

Three lessons:

1. Auto-contribution works for consistency. Manual/discretionary fails beyond 3 months. 2. Funds need a "next milestone" checkpoint inside the 12-month plan, not just the end-target date. 3. ETF-based funds need a longer runway; Wealth Glide Path's T-24mo rule is the right discipline.

Three priorities next year:

1. Re-anchor Down Payment with auto $500/mo. No more manual top-ups. 2. Vehicle Replacement with $150/mo auto + quarterly check-in. 3. Open Property Tax Fund — your peers miss this every first year of homeownership.

One structural change:

Move ETF-based Down Payment to 70/30 (ETF/short bond) instead of 100% ETF. Cuts volatility in half for ~1–2 ppt of expected return. With 3 years to target and a real deadline, this matters.

7. Neglected Fund Detector
📉Pairs with: Funding Gap Analyzer
The Prompt
You are scanning my funds for ones I stopped contributing to without realizing. Catch the neglected before the target date catches me. MY FUNDS (paste from Funding Gap Analyzer or Contribution Tracker): [Fund | Target | Target date | Current balance | Last contribution date | Last contribution amount | Required monthly to hit target] Look at each fund's contribution pattern. Flag any fund where: - LAST CONTRIBUTION was more than 60 days ago - TARGET DATE is approaching but FUNDING IS BEHIND - AUTO-CONTRIBUTION was set up but the transfer might have failed silently - LIFE EVENT (mentioned below) might have disrupted the contribution LIFE EVENTS THAT MIGHT HAVE DISRUPTED THIS YEAR: [brief — moved, job change, illness, big purchase, etc.] For each neglected fund: 1. WHEN it was last contributed to + how long since. 2. WHY (your best guess based on contribution pattern + life events I named). 3. STATUS — Recoverable / Date Slip Likely / Already Missed. 4. RECOVERY PLAN if recoverable — specific catch-up contribution path. 5. HONEST RECOMMENDATION if not — close the fund, defer the goal, or accept partial? Don't recommend "just contribute more." Be specific about what to do this month.
Worked Example
Example Input

Emergency Fund · $9,000 target · forever · $7,200 · last $200 (3wk ago) · monthly $200

Vacation · $3,000 · Jun 30 next yr · $2,800 · $200 (last mo) · monthly $200

Wedding Gift Fund · $1,500 · Sep 15 · $300 · $100 (8wk ago) · monthly $100

Vehicle Replacement · $15,000 · 4yr · $900 · $0 (5mo ago) · was monthly $150 first 4 mo

Pet Vet Fund · $1,800 · forever · $400 · $50 (4mo ago) · irregular $50

Life events this year: job change in May, moved apartments in July.

Example Output

1. Emergency Fund: ✅ Healthy. Last 3 wk ago, pattern intact.

2. Vacation: ✅ Healthy. Will hit target with mild buffer.

3. Wedding Gift Fund — ⚠️ Neglected. Last 8 weeks. Target ~2 months out. At $300/$1,500, need $600/mo to recover vs $100 pattern. Job change likely broke autopay; apartment move compounded.

Status: Date Slip Likely. Wedding isn't moving — either short the gift or pull from another fund.

Recommendation: Accept partial. Target $800–$1,000 (normal gift) vs $1,500. Catch up at $250/mo × 2 → hits $800 by Sep 15.

4. Vehicle Replacement — 🔴 Severely neglected. Last contribution 5 months ago — basically stopped after 4 months. Out of sight, out of mind + life events.

Status: Recoverable — 4-year horizon means catch-up is feasible. Re-anchor with $200/mo auto-contribution this week (vs original $150). Extra $50/mo recovers the gap over 18 months. Quarterly calendar reminder.

5. Pet Vet Fund — ⚠️ Neglected, low priority. Last 4 months. Irregular even before. Never on auto-contribution.

Recommendation: Either commit ($50/mo auto-pay) OR fold $400 into EF and close the fund. Half-existing funds imply coverage you don't actually have.

Two actions this month:
1. Set up auto-contribution to Vehicle Replacement Fund ($200/mo). One Tuesday afternoon.
2. Decide on Pet Vet — automate it or close it. Don't leave it half-existing.

Which AI should I use?

All seven prompts work in free tiers. Differences come down to output style + how each handles long structured data.

ChatGPT Free

Best for: Annual Fund Review, Life Stage Advisor, Metals Coach. Conversational tone; great at multi-part narratives + iterative editing ("make this shorter," "more specific to my situation").

Limit: ~3,000–4,000 word context per message. If you paste 15+ funds, do it in batches.

Claude Free (claude.ai)

Best for: Reallocation, Vehicle Advisor, Dividend Planner, Neglected Fund Detector. Better at table-formatted output, ranking lists, and structured per-fund analysis. Handles longer fund portfolios in one paste.

Limit: Daily conversation limit on free tier. If you hit it, switch to ChatGPT for the next prompt.

Paid tiers (ChatGPT Plus, Claude Pro)

Worth it if you're rebalancing monthly across 8+ funds OR running dividend analysis quarterly. Skip if checking in twice a year.

Universal tips
  1. Paste as text, not screenshots. Free-tier AI doesn't read spreadsheet screenshots reliably.
  2. Never paste account numbers or brokerage logins. Use the spreadsheet's fund names instead of account identifiers.
  3. Metals Coach works better with current data. Spot prices change daily — if you're rerunning after market hours, expect stale-data caveats.
  4. Save good output. Found a great fund-priority recommendation? Paste it into the matching fund's Notes column. Build your own playbook over the year.

Your fund data never leaves your AI conversation. The AI never sees your spreadsheet — only what you paste, only during that chat.
