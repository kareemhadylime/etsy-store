Page 2 · Intro + How to Use
How this library works.
Two halves. Front and back.
Variant
Life · 30pp

THE FRONT (workflows) is the unique bundle value — 10 cross-product workflows you can't run with a single spreadsheet. "Pay off debt while saving for a wedding." "Build a 10-year net-worth roadmap." "Launch a side business without breaking the personal budget." These are decisions that span products. The prompts chain together to make the decision-making concrete.

THE BACK (per-product reference) is the depth. 60+ prompts organized by spreadsheet, four to a page. Use them as you work in each product. Faster lookup than scrolling Reddit for prompt ideas.

How to use a workflow

Each workflow lists the products it touches at the top (color-coded chips). Inside the workflow: a short intro on when to run it, 3–5 prompts in sequence, copy-paste-ready, with [PLACEHOLDERS] for your data. A worked example shows what the chain produces end-to-end.

Run them in order. The output of one prompt feeds the next. If you only need part of a workflow, the prompts also work standalone.

How to use the per-product reference

Pages 15 onward. Each spreadsheet gets 2–3 pages. Each page has 4 prompts in a 2×2 grid: title, copy-paste prompt, the tab it pairs with. Use them à la carte.

Where these run

All prompts work in the free tier of your favourite AI assistant. Some workflows that weave 4+ products together with long data run better on a paid AI plan — flagged where it matters. See the Tips section on page 30 for guidance.

What this PDF won't do

It won't run prompts for you. You paste, you read, you decide. Your wedding, your business, your money, your call.

Turn the page when you're ready.

Workflow 1 of 10
Plan a year of sinking funds.
Turn a list of goals into a calendar of contributions.
Difficulty
Beginner
🪣 Sinking Funds
📊 Budget Tracker
When to use this. You know what you want to save for next year (Christmas, vacation, car repair, gift for parents' anniversary) but you're not sure how to split a fixed monthly savings budget across all of it. This workflow turns a list of goals into a calendar of contributions.
Prompt 1 / 3
Available-to-sink calculation
Given my budget data, calculate how much I have available each month for sinking funds. MONTHLY TAKE-HOME INCOME: $[amount] FIXED MONTHLY EXPENSES: [paste from Recurring Templates] VARIABLE EXPENSES (3-mo avg): [paste from Annual Summary] EMERGENCY FUND CONTRIBUTION: $[amount] DEBT MINIMUM PAYMENTS: $[amount] Tell me: (1) Total committed monthly spend, (2) Income minus committed = available to sink, (3) A reality-check buffer (5–10%) to leave unallocated. Just numbers. Brief reasoning. Skip the lecture.
Prompt 2 / 3
Goal ranking
Here are my sinking-fund goals for the next 12 months. Rank, allocate my available-to-sink budget, tell me which I can't fully fund. AVAILABLE MONTHLY: $[from Prompt 1] GOALS: [name | target | target date | priority hint] For each: (1) Months remaining, (2) Monthly contribution needed, (3) Cumulative monthly burden when added to higher-priority goals. Rank: must-fund > should-fund > nice-to-have. For any that don't fit, tell me whether to extend target date, reduce target, or drop the goal.
Prompt 3 / 3
Calendar generation
Build me a 12-month contribution calendar based on the ranking from the previous prompt. Format as table: rows = months, columns = funds. Add a "Total" row per month and verify against my available-to-sink budget. Include a "rollover" column showing unused budget that bleeds to the highest-priority underfunded goal. Flag any fund where a single-month spike is required and suggest an earlier start date.
Worked example
INPUT: $400/mo available. Goals: Christmas $1,200 by Dec, Vacation $2,400 by Jun, Car Repair $1,500 by Mar. OUTPUT: Jan: $100/$200/$100 = $400. Feb: $100/$200/$100 = $400. Mar: $100/$200/$100* — Car fully funded mid-Mar; rolls to Vacation. Jun: Vacation hits $2,400. Dec: Christmas hits $1,200. Flag: Vacation falls $0 short. Car Repair hits target 2 weeks before typical inspection date.

Workflow 2 of 10 · Wedding-specific
Pay off debt + save for wedding.
Both can't be your #1 priority — but the right split lets both progress.
Difficulty
Advanced
📉 Debt Payoff
🪣 Sinking Funds
💍 Wedding
When to use this. You're engaged. You have meaningful debt. You also have a wedding to pay for. This workflow finds the right split so both progress.
Prompt 1 / 3
Debt + wedding cost snapshot
Build a single-month financial picture. DEBT TOTAL: $[amount] · TOTAL MIN PAYMENTS: $[amount] · HIGHEST APR: [%] · LOWEST APR: [%] · DEBT-FREE DATE AT MIN: [date] WEDDING BUDGET: $[total] · WEDDING DATE: [date] · ALREADY SAVED: $[amount] · WEDDING GAP: $[delta] EXTRA AVAILABLE EACH MONTH: $[amount] Tell me, in numbers: (1) Months until wedding, (2) Monthly wedding saving rate needed, (3) Monthly extra-to-debt at current trajectory, (4) Debt-free date shift if I dropped the wedding entirely. Baseline data only. Don't recommend yet.
Prompt 2 / 3
Split the extra
Propose three splits of my "extra available" between debt and wedding: OPTION A — Aggressive wedding (80% wedding / 20% debt) OPTION B — Balanced (50/50) OPTION C — Aggressive debt (20% wedding / 80% debt) For each: (1) Will wedding be fully funded by wedding date? (Y/N + gap), (2) New debt-free date, (3) Total interest paid, (4) Emotional/relationship trade-off in one line. Then recommend one option. Brief reasoning.
Prompt 3 / 3
Stretch-month strategy
Identify months where I likely have extra income (bonus, tax refund, side gig, gift) and propose how to deploy those windfalls. EXPECTED WINDFALLS: Tax refund $[amount] in [month]; Annual bonus $[amount] in [month]; Other [describe] For each: 100% to debt (and date impact), 100% to wedding (and gap impact), or split %. Default rule: if highest-APR debt > 8%, lean windfall-to-debt; else lean wedding. Override only with specific reason.
Worked example
INPUT: $12k debt at 19% APR · Wedding $25k in 14 mo · $8k saved · $500/mo extra. Months until wedding: 14. Wedding rate needed: ($25k − $8k)/14 = $1,214/mo (exceeds $500). Option A: $400 wedding + $100 debt → wedding gap $11.4k at wedding date. Option B: $250/$250 → wedding gap $13.5k. Option C: $100/$400 → wedding gap $15.6k. RECOMMENDATION: None fully funds wedding. Either (a) reduce target ~$10k, (b) extend date 8+ mo, or (c) accept partial financing.

Intermediate
📊 Budget Tracker
💰 Net Worth Tracker
When to use this. Right after the wedding, or anytime two people consolidate their money.
Prompt 1 / 3
Merge the budgets
Merge two pre-marriage budgets into one. Flag conflicts. PERSON A BUDGET: [list with $] · PERSON B BUDGET: [list with $] · JOINT INCOME: $[combined monthly] For each category: if both have it, propose new joint target (sum is rarely right). If only one has it, decide keep or merge. Flag conflicts (e.g., "Gym $80 for A, $0 for B"). Suggest 3–5 NEW joint categories neither had (date nights, future home repairs, holiday hosting). Prompt 2 / 3
Joint vs. individual expenses
Some expenses stay individual (hobbies, personal therapy, friend gifts). Others are joint. Draw the line. CATEGORIES (from Prompt 1): [list] Classify each: JOINT (joint account, both visible) · INDIVIDUAL (each gets fixed allowance, no justification) · HYBRID (joint default, opt-out personal spend within). Recommend individual allowance per person (typically 5–15% of after-fixed-costs income each).
Prompt 3 / 3
Combine net worth
Merge two Net Worth statements. Flag imbalances. PERSON A ASSETS + LIABILITIES: [data] · PERSON B ASSETS + LIABILITIES: [data] Show: (1) Combined NW, (2) Asset class allocation post-merge, (3) Liability mix + combined debt-to-asset ratio, (4) Allocation imbalance (90% retirement on one side, 90% cash on other is a flag). Recommend 3 first-30-days actions. Concrete steps. Skip "consult a financial advisor."
Worked example
INPUT: A: Rent $1,200, Groceries $400, Subs $80, Gym $60. B: Rent $1,400, Groceries $500, Books $40, Phone $50. OUTPUT: Rent → $1,800 new place (both lose individual rents). Groceries → $700 joint. Subs → $80 (A keeps Spotify Family covering both). Gym → Individual A allowance. Phone → $100 family plan. Books → Individual B allowance. NEW JOINT: Date nights $150, Home repairs SF $100, Holiday hosting $50.

Workflow 4 of 10
Launch a side business — keep personal budget intact.
The first 6 months are usually a loss. Keep personal finances safe while the business finds footing.
Difficulty
Advanced
🏢 Small Business
📊 Budget Tracker
💰 Net Worth Tracker
When to use this. You want to start something on the side (consulting, Etsy shop, freelance, productized service).
Prompt 1 / 3
Startup cost separation
Separate startup costs from personal budget — don't cannibalize household money. ONE-TIME STARTUP COSTS: [list with $] MONTHLY RECURRING BUSINESS COSTS: [list with $] CURRENT MONTHLY HOUSEHOLD SURPLUS: $[amount] Tell me: (1) Total one-time, (2) Total ongoing monthly, (3) Max business outflow I can absorb without dipping into EF or savings, (4) Recommended "business escrow" amount = pool to cover first 6 months ops + buffer. Format: "You can absorb $X/mo. Pre-fund $Y for 6 mo. Don't exceed $Z one-time without external financing."
Prompt 2 / 3
Go/no-go revenue thresholds
Set three revenue milestones with explicit go/no-go decisions. Monthly business cost (Prompt 1): $[amount] · Acceptable monthly loss first 6 mo: $[default $200] · Revenue target by month 12: $[amount] Build three milestones: MILESTONE 1 (Month 3) — minimum revenue to continue · MILESTONE 2 (Month 6) — break-even check · MILESTONE 3 (Month 12) — contribution-margin check. For each: threshold AND specific action if under (cut, pivot, wind down, continue). Be specific about wind-down trigger.
Prompt 3 / 3
Tax-prep separation from Day 1
Set up tax separation Day 1 to avoid a Year-1 nightmare. STRUCTURE: [sole prop / LLC / S-corp] · STATE: [state] · YEAR-1 EXPECTED REV: $[amount] · SEPARATE BANK: [yes/no] Give me: (1) Three things to do this week for clean separation, (2) Expense categories with explicit IRS Schedule mapping (Schedule C), (3) Quarterly estimated tax dates + how to estimate first payment with limited history, (4) One thing Year-1 owners typically screw up that I should preempt. Practical. Skip "consult a CPA" — tell me how to set this up myself.
Worked example
INPUT: Etsy shop, $800 startup, $120/mo recurring, $600/mo household surplus. Absorb up to $400/mo without touching EF. Pre-fund $720 (6 × $120) + $300 buffer = $1,020. Don't exceed $800 one-time. Milestones: M3 $300 rev — continue. M6 $120/mo break-even — must hit; else cut Canva to free, pivot. M12 $500/mo gross + 30% margin = $150 profit — minimum for "worth my time." If M6 misses: wind down. Refund inventory, downgrade subs, write off as learning.

Workflow 5 of 10
Build a 10-year net-worth roadmap.
Long-term decisions need a long-term view.
Difficulty
Advanced
💰 Net Worth Tracker
📉 Debt Payoff
📈 Investment (ref)
When to use this. Project net worth 10 years out under three savings/debt-paydown strategies, then highlight milestones every 2 years.
Prompt 1 / 3
Baseline projection
Project net worth 10 years out at three savings rates. CURRENT NW: $[amount] · MONTHLY SAVINGS: $[amount] · EXPECTED REAL RETURN (use 7% if unsure): [%] · CURRENT DEBT: $[amount] · DEBT-FREE DATE: [date] Project NW end-of-year for 10 years under: SCENARIO A maintain current rate · SCENARIO B +25% rate · SCENARIO C +50% rate. Show me the curve, not just year-10 number. Where does compounding inflect (year where annual returns exceed annual contributions)?
Prompt 2 / 3
Debt-fast vs. invest-difference vs. hybrid
Three strategies. Same 10-year window. Project under each. S1 DEBT FAST: all extra to highest-APR debt until debt-free, then invest aggressively. S2 INVEST DIFFERENCE: pay only debt minimums, invest the difference at [%]. S3 HYBRID: 60% to highest-APR debt, 40% to investments concurrently. For each: year-by-year NW (table), total interest paid, total investment value at year 10, risk level + one-line rationale. Recommend one based on my highest debt APR. Rule of thumb: if APR > expected return + 2%, pay fast. Else, hybrid.
Prompt 3 / 3
Milestone map (every 2 years)
Build a milestone map for the next 10 years — one milestone per 2 years. Use the recommended strategy from Prompt 2. Milestones to include: Y2 short-term goal (EF full, debt #1 paid off) · Y4 mid-term goal (debt-free, first house DP hit) · Y6 wealth-building (passive income covers 1 fixed cost) · Y8 stretch (investment account hits meaningful number) · Y10 FIRE-progress check. For each: target number, "earliest realistic" date based on projection, one leading indicator to monitor quarterly.
Worked example
INPUT: NW $40k · Savings $500/mo · 7% return · Debt $15k at 18% APR. S-A: Year 10 NW = $128k. S-B (+25%): $146k. S-C (+50%): $164k. Strategy 1 (Debt fast): $138k, interest $2.1k, risk low. Strategy 2 (Invest diff): $119k, interest $9.4k, risk high (APR > return). Strategy 3 (Hybrid 60/40): $131k, interest $4.8k, risk med. RECOMMENDATION: Strategy 1. 18% well above 7% — killing debt first wins by Y10. MILESTONES: Y2 debt-free + $15k EF. Y4 $40k investments. Y6 $75k investments, passive income covers phone+internet. Y8 $115k Coast FIRE check. Y10 $138k.

Workflow 6 of 10
Pay off debt vs. invest the difference.
Specific decision. Run the math for your APR, return, and timeline.
Difficulty
Intermediate
📉 Debt Payoff
💰 Net Worth Tracker
When to use this. You have extra cash. Should it go to debt or investments?
Prompt 1 / 3
The math
I have $[amount]/mo extra. Should I deploy to debt or investments? HIGHEST-APR DEBT: Balance $[amount] · APR [%] · Min payment $[amount] INVESTMENT: Account [taxable / Roth / 401k] · Expected real return [%, default 7] · Marginal tax bracket [%] TIME HORIZON: 10 years. Assume no 401k match left on the table. Compare in real (inflation-adjusted) terms: (1) Year-10 net wealth if I pay extra to debt first then invest the freed cash, (2) Year-10 net wealth if I pay only minimums and invest the extra. Account for after-tax cost of debt interest (most consumer debt non-deductible).
Prompt 2 / 3
Decision framework
Give me the decision rule, not just the math. Build a 4-question yes/no flowchart that ends in "Pay Debt First" / "Invest the Difference" / "Hybrid 60/40": (1) APR threshold — below this, lean investments (2) Volatility tolerance — can I stomach 15% drawdowns while paying debt interest? (3) Psychological factor — is "debt-free" feeling worth more than 1–2% wealth edge? (4) Liquidity — does investing instead of paying leave me without a buffer?
Prompt 3 / 3
90-day action plan
Whichever path I chose, give me a 90-day action plan. WEEK 1: [specific action] · WEEK 2: [specific action] · ... · DAY 90: [check-in metric] If "Pay Debt First": order of debts (snowball vs. avalanche), monthly extra amount. If "Invest the Difference": account choice (Roth vs. taxable), allocation, auto-invest setup. If "Hybrid": split the actions across both tracks.
Worked example
INPUT: $400/mo extra · Highest debt $8k at 6.5% APR · Expected return 7% · 10-yr horizon. MATH: Year-10 NW "pay debt first" $54.3k. "Invest difference" $54.8k. Effectively a wash (investing edges by $0.5k). DECISION TREE: Q1 APR (6.5%) > return (7%)? No → lean invest. Q2 tolerate 15% drawdowns? Yes → lean invest. Q3 value debt-free? Maybe → marginally leans debt. Q4 sufficient liquidity? Yes → lean invest. RECOMMENDED: HYBRID 60% INVEST / 40% DEBT. Gap too narrow to be doctrinaire; emotional factor favors keeping debt moving. 90-DAY: Week 1 auto-invest $240/mo Roth IRA index. Week 2 extra debt $160/mo auto-pay. Day 90 review.

Workflow 7 of 10 · Wedding-specific
Wedding-budget reverse-engineering.
From total → per-vendor. Using regional benchmarks and your priorities.
Difficulty
Intermediate
💍 Wedding
📊 Budget Tracker
When to use this. You know your wedding budget total. You don't know what to spend per vendor.
Prompt 1 / 3
Priority weighting
I have a wedding budget but don't know how to allocate across categories. TOTAL: $[amount] · GUEST COUNT: [number] · REGION: [city, state] · SEASON: [spring/summer/fall/winter] Rank these 1 (most important) to 5 (least): Venue · Catering+bar · Photo+video · Attire · Flowers+decor · Music · Stationery · Hair+makeup · Officiant · Transport · Favors · Contingency. PRIORITIES (paste your ranks): [...] Suggest an allocation % per category matching MY priorities, not "venue is 50%" rule unless Venue rank is 1. Respect MY priorities, not magazine average.
Prompt 2 / 3
Regional reality-check
Check the allocation from Prompt 1 against actual regional pricing. For each category with $ allocation: (1) Realistic for [region] in [season] for [guest count]? (2) If under-allocated, flag + tell me typical. (3) If over-allocated, suggest swap-outs (DIY vs. hire, in-season flowers, less elaborate stationery). End with a "realistic total" respecting regional minimums. If my total can't cover minimums, name 1–3 categories to cut hardest.
Prompt 3 / 3
Contingency + swap-out plan
Build a 5–10% contingency buffer + swap-out plan for categories I might cut. CONTINGENCY: 5–10% of total. Hold separate. Don't allocate to any specific category. SWAP-OUT PLAN: For each of the 3 highest-cost categories, give me one $200–$500 swap-out I could trigger if a vendor surprises me later (e.g., "DIY ceremony arrangements instead of pro florist for ceremony only — saves ~$400"). Stack swap-outs from least painful to most. Easy ones queued = no scrambling in month 8.
Worked example
INPUT: $25k total · 100 guests · Austin TX, October · Priorities: Photo #1, Venue #2, Catering #3. ALLOCATION: Photo 14% ($3,500), Venue 32% ($8,000), Catering 26% ($6,500), Attire 8% ($2,000), Music 6% ($1,500), Flowers 5% ($1,250)... REGIONAL: Catering $80-120/head = $8-12k → under by $1.5-5.5k. Recommendation: shift $2k from "general" to catering. CONTINGENCY: $1,500 (6%). SWAP-OUTS queued: (1) DIY ceremony flowers ~$400, (2) Buffet vs. plated ~$1k, (3) Friend with PA system ~$1.5k.

Prompt 1 / 3
Cross-product snapshot
Build a quarterly snapshot. Don't analyze — consolidate. PERIOD: [Q1 2026 / etc.] · DATE RANGE: [start] to [end] From each spreadsheet, give me the headline: - Budget — actual vs. budget % + Top 3 over-categories - Debt Payoff — total debt change ($Δ + %) + debt-free date shift - Sinking Funds — % of funded goals on track + flagged risks - Net Worth — NW change ($Δ + %) + driver (savings/market/debt paydown) - Small Business (if applicable) — revenue, gross profit, cash - Wedding (if applicable) — budget used vs. timeline, RSVP % Format: 2-column table Spreadsheet | Headline. Next-quarter priorities
Set 5 priorities for next quarter. Each priority: (1) Specific (not "save more" — "hit $4,000 in sinking funds by July 1"), (2) Measurable, (3) Attached to a spreadsheet to track in, (4) Trade-off explicit. Order by which is most fragile (most likely to fall off if not defended). End with one "watch this" item — metric to monitor but not yet a priority.

WATCH: Joint income (spouse considering job change).

Workflow 10 of 10
"What changed this month?"
First of every month. Five minutes. Anomaly detection across all 6.
Difficulty

📊 Budget
📉 Debt
🪣 Sinking

When to use this. First of every month. AI-powered anomaly detection across all six spreadsheets.
Prompt 1 / 3
Detect anomalies
Compare this month vs. baseline (last 3-mo avg OR last year same month — whichever I have data for). Surface what's materially different. THIS MONTH ([month, year]): Budget headlines · Debt Payoff · Sinking Funds · Net Worth · Small Business (if app) · Wedding (if app) LAST 3 MONTH AVG: [paste] For each category, flag: Material variance (>15% delta) — POSITIVE or NEGATIVE · One-time vs. recurring · Pattern continuation (3rd month?) vs. one-month deviation. Output: single table Item | This Mo | Baseline | Δ% | Type.
Prompt 2 / 3
Explain the why
For each material variance, propose 2–3 plausible explanations. VARIANCES (from Prompt 1): [list] For each: (1) Most likely cause (seasonality, holidays), (2) Alternative cause (if behavior changed), (3) "Weirder" cause to consider (sub auto-renewal that doubled). End each with Yes/No question to confirm. Don't speculate beyond what data supports.
Prompt 3 / 3
Action items
Convert variances + explanations into a short action list. For each material variance, tag: - IGNORE: known one-off, no action - INVESTIGATE: specific thing to check (open statement, verify $340 charge on the 14th) - FIX: recurring issue to change (cancel duplicate sub) - WATCH: keep monitoring before acting Output: 3–5 item bulleted action list. Skip items with no action.
Worked example
VARIANCES (October 2026 vs. trailing 3-mo avg): Dining $420 vs $245 +71% one-off · Subs $134 vs $89 +51% recurring (3rd mo) · Vacation contrib $0 vs $250 -100% one-off · NW +$680 vs $1,800 -62% one-off · Side biz rev $720 vs $420 +71% pattern (2nd mo). NW — market down month. One-off. Side biz — holiday pickup. Pattern. ACTIONS: (1) INVESTIGATE: cloud storage auto-renewal — cancel/downgrade. (2) FIX: resume Vacation Nov 1 ($300/mo to catch up). Where to run these prompts.
AI assistants and Notion AI — strengths, limits, when each wins.
Tier guide
Free + Paid
Free tier — conversational
Best for: speech drafts, vendor negotiations, RSVP scripts, vows, prompt iteration. Conversational tone, fast revisions.

Limit: ~3,000-4,000 word context per single message. If you paste a 200-name guest list, split it: batch 1, analysis, batch 2, ask AI to combine.

Most assistants offer a capable free tier as of 2026, with limited daily access to their strongest model.
Free tier — long structured input
Best for: guest list optimization, seating constraints, vendor cost intelligence, anything with longer input + structured table output.

Limit: daily message limit on free tier. Wait or switch to another AI assistant for the next prompt.

Strength: some assistants handle very long pastes in a single message — useful for cross-product workflows when pasting from 3+ spreadsheets.
Notion AI
Best for: if you also own Notion Life OS. Notion AI lives inside your workspace. Most per-product reference prompts paste directly into a Notion AI block.

Cost: $10/mo add-on to Notion. Worth it if using the Notion Life OS template — Notion AI reads your databases in-context.

Doesn't: anything a general-purpose AI assistant does better with full context (10-yr projections). Use for in-context Notion tasks only.
Paid tier — worth it?
Worth it if: using these prompts heavily over 2-3 months (active wedding planning, year-end tax prep, post-marriage finance merge).

Paid tiers run faster, allow larger uploads (helpful for cross-product workflows), have higher daily limits.

Skip if: use is occasional. Free tiers do the job for individual prompts.
Universal tips
  1. Paste data as text, not screenshots. AI in free tiers can't read images well. Copy cells from your spreadsheet, paste as text.
  2. Read the worked example first. It tells you what good output looks like. If yours doesn't match, you probably missed a placeholder.
  3. Iterate. Rarely is the first response perfect. "Make it 30 words shorter" / "be more direct" — AI revises on request.
  4. Save what works. Found a great paragraph in iteration 3? Copy it into your spreadsheet's Notes column. Otherwise it disappears with the conversation.
  5. Never paste account numbers, full SSN, or passwords. Data lives in the AI service for the conversation (and possibly longer per their privacy policy). Use placeholders for sensitive numbers.
  6. Workflows beat standalone prompts for big decisions. The 10 cross-product workflows are the bundle's unique value. Use a workflow for real decisions.
