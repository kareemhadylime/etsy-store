Page 2 · Intro + How to Use
How this library works.
Two halves. Front and back.
Variant
Finance · 28pp

THE FRONT (workflows) is the unique bundle value — 10 cross-product workflows you can't run with a single spreadsheet. "Pay off debt while saving for a major goal." "Build a 10-year net-worth roadmap." "Launch a side business without breaking the personal budget." These are decisions that span products. The prompts chain together to make the decision-making concrete.

THE BACK (per-product reference) is the depth. 60 prompts organized by spreadsheet, four to a page. Use them as you work in each product. Faster lookup than scrolling Reddit for prompt ideas.

How to use a workflow

Each workflow lists the products it touches at the top (color-coded chips). Inside the workflow: a short intro on when to run it, 3–5 prompts in sequence, copy-paste-ready, with [PLACEHOLDERS] for your data. A worked example shows what the chain produces end-to-end.

Run them in order. The output of one prompt feeds the next. If you only need part of a workflow, the prompts also work standalone.

How to use the per-product reference

Pages 15 onward. Each spreadsheet gets 2–3 pages. Each page has 4 prompts in a 2×2 grid: title, copy-paste prompt, the tab it pairs with. Use them à la carte.

Where these run

All prompts work in ChatGPT's free tier or Claude's free tier. Some workflows that weave 4+ products together with long data run better in paid tiers — flagged where it matters. See the Tips section on page 28 for guidance.

What this PDF won't do

It won't run prompts for you. You paste, you read, you decide. Your money, your call.

Turn the page when you're ready.

Workflow 1 of 10
Plan a year of sinking funds.
Turn a list of goals into a calendar of contributions.
Difficulty
Beginner
🪣 Sinking Funds
📊 Budget Tracker
When to use this. You know what you want to save for next year but you're not sure how to split a fixed monthly savings budget. This workflow turns a list of goals into a calendar of contributions.
Prompt 1 / 3
Available-to-sink calculation
Given my budget data, calculate how much I have available each month for sinking funds. MONTHLY TAKE-HOME INCOME: $[amount] FIXED MONTHLY EXPENSES: [paste from Recurring Templates] VARIABLE EXPENSES (3-mo avg): [paste from Annual Summary] EMERGENCY FUND CONTRIBUTION: $[amount] DEBT MINIMUM PAYMENTS: $[amount] Tell me: (1) Total committed monthly spend, (2) Income minus committed = available to sink, (3) A reality-check buffer (5-10%) to leave unallocated. Just numbers. Brief reasoning. Skip the lecture.
Prompt 2 / 3
Goal ranking
Here are my sinking-fund goals. Rank, allocate, tell me which I can't fully fund. AVAILABLE MONTHLY: $[from Prompt 1] GOALS: [name | target | target date | priority hint] For each: (1) Months remaining, (2) Monthly contribution needed, (3) Cumulative monthly burden when added to higher-priority goals. Rank: must-fund > should-fund > nice-to-have. For any that don't fit: extend date, reduce target, or drop.
Prompt 3 / 3
Calendar generation
Build a 12-month contribution calendar based on the ranking from Prompt 2. Format as table: rows = months, columns = funds. Add a "Total" row per month and verify against my available-to-sink. Include a "rollover" column showing unused budget that bleeds to highest-priority underfunded goal. Flag any fund needing a single-month spike and suggest an earlier start date.
Worked example
INPUT: $400/mo available. Goals: Christmas $1,200 by Dec, Vacation $2,400 by Jun, Car Repair $1,500 by Mar. OUTPUT: Jan $100/$200/$100 = $400. Feb same. Mar Car fully funded mid-Mar; rolls to Vacation. Jun Vacation hits $2,400. Dec Christmas hits $1,200. Flag: Vacation hits target. Car Repair 2 weeks before typical inspection date.

Workflow 2 of 10
Pay off debt + save for a major goal.
Both can't be your #1 priority — but the right split lets both progress.
Difficulty
Advanced
📉 Debt Payoff
🪣 Sinking Funds
📊 Budget Tracker
When to use this. You have meaningful debt and a major savings goal (down payment, vehicle, business launch fund, sabbatical). This workflow finds the right split so both progress.
Prompt 1 / 3
Debt + goal cost snapshot
Build a single-month financial picture. DEBT TOTAL: $[amount] · TOTAL MIN PAYMENTS: $[amount] · HIGHEST APR: [%] · LOWEST APR: [%] · DEBT-FREE DATE AT MIN: [date] GOAL: [describe — house DP / vehicle / business launch / sabbatical] · TARGET AMOUNT: $[total] · TARGET DATE: [date] · ALREADY SAVED: $[amount] · GAP: $[delta] EXTRA AVAILABLE EACH MONTH: $[amount] Tell me, in numbers: (1) Months until goal, (2) Monthly saving rate needed, (3) Monthly extra-to-debt at current trajectory, (4) Debt-free date shift if I dropped the goal entirely. Baseline data only. Don't recommend yet.
Prompt 2 / 3
Split the extra
Propose three splits of my "extra available" between debt and goal: OPTION A — Aggressive goal (80% goal / 20% debt) OPTION B — Balanced (50/50) OPTION C — Aggressive debt (20% goal / 80% debt) For each: (1) Will goal be fully funded by target date? (Y/N + gap), (2) New debt-free date, (3) Total interest paid, (4) Trade-off in one line. Then recommend one. Brief reasoning.
Prompt 3 / 3
Stretch-month strategy
Identify months where I likely have extra income (bonus, tax refund, side gig, gift) and propose how to deploy. EXPECTED WINDFALLS: Tax refund $[amount] in [month]; Annual bonus $[amount] in [month]; Other [describe] For each: 100% to debt (and date impact), 100% to goal (and gap impact), or split %. Default rule: if highest-APR debt > 8%, lean windfall-to-debt; else lean goal. Override only with specific reason.
Worked example
INPUT: $12k debt at 19% APR · Goal: $20k down payment in 14 mo · $8k saved · $500/mo extra. Months until goal: 14. Saving rate needed: ($20k − $8k)/14 = $857/mo (exceeds $500). Option A: $400 goal + $100 debt → goal gap $6.4k at target date. Option B: $250/$250 → goal gap $8.5k. Option C: $100/$400 → goal gap $10.6k. RECOMMENDATION: None fully funds goal. Either (a) reduce target ~$5k, (b) extend date 6+ mo, or (c) accept partial gap.

📊 Budget Tracker
💰 Net Worth Tracker
When to use this. Any time two people consolidate their money — newly married, moving in together, formalizing a long-term partnership.
Prompt 1 / 3
Merge the budgets
Merge two pre-existing budgets into one. Flag conflicts. PERSON A BUDGET: [list with $] · PERSON B BUDGET: [list with $] · JOINT INCOME: $[combined monthly] For each category: if both have it, propose new joint target (sum is rarely right). If only one has it, decide keep or merge. Flag conflicts. Suggest 3-5 NEW joint categories neither had (date nights, future home repairs, holiday hosting). Format: Joint Category | A old | B old | New joint | Notes.
Prompt 2 / 3
Joint vs. individual expenses
Some expenses stay individual (hobbies, friend gifts). Others are joint. Draw the line. CATEGORIES (from Prompt 1): [list] Classify each: JOINT (joint account, both visible) · INDIVIDUAL (each gets fixed allowance) · HYBRID (joint default, opt-out personal spend within). Recommend individual allowance per person (typically 5-15% of after-fixed-costs income each).
Prompt 3 / 3
Combine net worth
Merge two Net Worth statements. Flag imbalances. PERSON A ASSETS + LIABILITIES: [data] · PERSON B ASSETS + LIABILITIES: [data] Show: (1) Combined NW, (2) Asset class allocation post-merge, (3) Liability mix + combined debt-to-asset ratio, (4) Allocation imbalance (90% retirement on one side, 90% cash on other is a flag). Recommend 3 first-30-days actions. Concrete steps. Skip "consult a financial advisor."
Worked example
INPUT: A: Rent $1,200, Groceries $400, Subs $80. B: Rent $1,400, Groceries $500, Books $40. OUTPUT: Rent → $1,800 new place. Groceries → $700 joint. Subs → $80 (A keeps Spotify Family). Books → Individual B allowance. NEW JOINT: Date nights $150, Home repairs SF $100, Holiday hosting $50.

When to use this. You want to start something on the side (consulting, Etsy shop, freelance, productized service).
Prompt 1 / 3
Startup cost separation
Separate startup costs from personal budget. ONE-TIME STARTUP COSTS: [list with $] MONTHLY RECURRING BUSINESS COSTS: [list with $] CURRENT MONTHLY HOUSEHOLD SURPLUS: $[amount] Tell me: (1) Total one-time, (2) Total ongoing monthly, (3) Max business outflow I can absorb without dipping into EF, (4) Recommended "business escrow" amount = pool to cover first 6 months ops + buffer. Prompt 2 / 3
Go/no-go revenue thresholds
Three revenue milestones with explicit go/no-go decisions. Monthly business cost: $[amount] · Acceptable monthly loss first 6 mo: $[default $200] · Revenue target by mo 12: $[amount] Build: M3 minimum to continue · M6 break-even check · M12 contribution-margin check. For each: threshold AND specific action if under (cut, pivot, wind down, continue). Be specific about wind-down trigger.
Prompt 3 / 3
Tax-prep separation from Day 1
Set up tax separation Day 1 to avoid Year-1 nightmare. STRUCTURE: [sole prop / LLC / S-corp] · STATE: [state] · Y1 EXPECTED REV: $[amount] · SEPARATE BANK: [yes/no] Give me: (1) Three things to do this week, (2) Expense categories with IRS Schedule mapping, (3) Quarterly estimated tax dates + first-payment estimate, (4) One thing Year-1 owners typically screw up. Practical. Skip "consult a CPA" — tell me how to set this up myself.
Worked example
INPUT: Etsy shop, $800 startup, $120/mo recurring, $600/mo household surplus. Absorb $400/mo. Pre-fund $720 (6 × $120) + $300 = $1,020. Don't exceed $800 one-time. Milestones: M3 $300 rev continue. M6 $120/mo break-even must hit; else cut Canva, pivot. M12 $500/mo gross + 30% margin = $150 profit. If M6 misses: wind down. Refund inventory, downgrade subs, write off as learning.

Workflow 5 of 10
Build a 10-year net-worth roadmap.
Long-term decisions need a long-term view.
Difficulty
Advanced
💰 Net Worth Tracker
📉 Debt Payoff
📈 Investment (ref)
When to use this. Project net worth 10 years out under three savings/debt-paydown strategies, then highlight milestones every 2 years.
Prompt 1 / 3
Baseline projection
Project net worth 10 years out at three savings rates. CURRENT NW: $[amount] · MONTHLY SAVINGS: $[amount] · EXPECTED REAL RETURN: 7% · CURRENT DEBT: $[amount] · DEBT-FREE DATE: [date] Project NW end-of-year for 10 years under: SCENARIO A maintain · SCENARIO B +25% rate · SCENARIO C +50% rate. Show me the curve. Where does compounding inflect (year where annual returns exceed annual contributions)?
Prompt 2 / 3
Debt-fast vs. invest-difference vs. hybrid
Three strategies. Same 10-year window. S1 DEBT FAST · S2 INVEST DIFFERENCE (pay minimums only, invest the difference at [%]) · S3 HYBRID 60% debt / 40% invest. For each: year-by-year NW table, total interest paid, total investment value at year 10, risk level + one-sentence rationale. Recommend one based on my highest debt APR. Rule of thumb: if APR > expected return + 2%, pay fast. Else, hybrid.
Prompt 3 / 3
Milestone map (every 2 years)
Build a milestone map: Y2 short-term · Y4 mid-term · Y6 wealth-building · Y8 stretch · Y10 FIRE check. For each: target number, "earliest realistic" date, one leading indicator to monitor quarterly.
Worked example
INPUT: NW $40k · Savings $500/mo · 7% return · Debt $15k at 18% APR. S-A: Y10 NW $128k. S-B (+25%): $146k. S-C (+50%): $164k. Strategy 1 (Debt fast): $138k, interest $2.1k, low risk. Strategy 2 (Invest diff): $119k, interest $9.4k, high risk (APR > return). Strategy 3 (Hybrid 60/40): $131k. RECOMMENDATION: Strategy 1. 18% well above 7% — killing debt first wins. MILESTONES: Y2 debt-free + $15k EF · Y4 $40k investments · Y6 $75k, passive covers phone+internet · Y8 $115k Coast FIRE · Y10 $138k.

Workflow 6 of 10
Pay off debt vs. invest the difference.
Run the math for your APR, return, and timeline.
Difficulty
Intermediate
📉 Debt Payoff
💰 Net Worth Tracker
When to use this. You have extra cash. Should it go to debt or investments?
Prompt 1 / 3
The math
$[amount]/mo extra. Debt or invest? HIGHEST-APR DEBT: Balance $[n] · APR [%] · Min $[n] INVESTMENT: [taxable/Roth/401k] · Expected real return [%, default 7] · Marginal tax bracket [%] HORIZON: 10 years. No 401k match left on table. Compare in real terms: (1) Y10 net wealth if I pay extra to debt then invest freed cash, (2) Y10 net wealth if I pay only minimums and invest the extra. Account for after-tax cost of debt interest (most consumer debt non-deductible).
Prompt 2 / 3
Decision framework
Build a 4-question yes/no flowchart that ends in "Pay Debt First" / "Invest the Difference" / "Hybrid 60/40": (1) APR threshold — below this, lean investments (2) Volatility tolerance — can I stomach 15% drawdowns while paying debt interest? (3) Psychological factor — is "debt-free" worth more than 1-2% wealth edge? (4) Liquidity — does investing instead of paying leave me without buffer?
Prompt 3 / 3
90-day action plan
Whichever path: 90-day plan. WEEK 1: [specific action] · ... · DAY 90: [check-in metric] If "Pay Debt First": order of debts (snowball vs avalanche), monthly extra amount. If "Invest Difference": account choice (Roth vs taxable), allocation, auto-invest setup. If "Hybrid": split across both tracks.
Worked example
INPUT: $400/mo extra · Highest debt $8k at 6.5% APR · Return 7% · 10-yr horizon. MATH: Y10 NW "pay first" $54.3k. "Invest diff" $54.8k. Wash (investing edges $0.5k). DECISION: Q1 APR (6.5%) > return (7%)? No → lean invest. Q2 tolerate 15% drawdowns? Yes → invest. Q3 value debt-free? Maybe → marginally debt. Q4 sufficient liquidity? Yes → invest. RECOMMENDED: HYBRID 60% INVEST / 40% DEBT. 90-DAY: W1 auto-invest $240/mo Roth IRA index. W2 extra debt $160/mo auto-pay. Day 90 review.

Workflow 7 of 10
Reverse-engineer a major savings goal.
From total → monthly allocations. Using benchmarks and your priorities.
Difficulty
Intermediate
🪣 Sinking Funds
📊 Budget Tracker
When to use this. You know your total savings target (down payment, sabbatical, business launch, vehicle, multi-year travel). You don't know how to break it into monthly contributions across the planning window.
Prompt 1 / 3
Priority weighting + sub-budgets
I have a major savings goal. Break it into sub-budgets matching my priorities. TOTAL TARGET: $[amount] · TARGET DATE: [date] · GOAL TYPE: [house DP / vehicle / sabbatical / business launch / multi-year travel / other] For each typical sub-budget within this goal type (e.g., for a house: DP + closing costs + emergency reserves + immediate furnishing + moving), give priority rank 1 (essential) to 5 (nice-to-have). PRIORITY RANKS (paste yours): [...] Suggest an allocation % per sub-budget that matches MY priorities, not a generic rule. Respect MY priorities.
Prompt 2 / 3
Reality-check the allocation
Check the allocation from Prompt 1 against realistic pricing. REGION / CONTEXT: [city, state / business industry / etc.] For each sub-budget with $ allocation: (1) Realistic for context? (2) If under-allocated, flag + tell me typical. (3) If over-allocated, suggest alternatives (cheaper paths, phased approach, DIY vs hire). End with a "realistic total" respecting minimums. If my total can't cover minimums, name 1-3 sub-budgets to cut hardest.
Prompt 3 / 3
Contingency + swap-out plan
Build a 5-10% contingency buffer + swap-out plan. CONTINGENCY: 5-10% of total. Hold separate. Don't allocate to a specific sub-budget. SWAP-OUT PLAN: For each of the 3 highest-cost sub-budgets, give me one $200-$500 swap-out I could trigger if a vendor / cost surprises me later. Stack from least painful to most. Easy ones queued = no scrambling later.
Worked example
INPUT: $50k goal — house DP in Austin TX, 24-month window. ALLOCATION: DP 70% ($35k), Closing 12% ($6k), Reserves 10% ($5k), Furnishing 6% ($3k), Moving 2% ($1k). REGIONAL CHECK: Austin starter home ~$350k. 20% DP = $70k. Your $35k = 10% DP → PMI required. Recommend either (a) reduce home target to ~$250k for 20% DP, (b) accept PMI for first 2-3 years. CONTINGENCY: $3k (6%). SWAP-OUTS queued: (1) Skip professional movers ~$800, (2) Used furniture for first year ~$1.5k, (3) Smaller-market suburb cuts price ~$30k.

Detect anomalies
Compare this month vs baseline (last 3-mo avg OR last year same month). THIS MONTH ([month, year]): Budget · Debt · Sinking · NW · Small Biz (if app) LAST 3 MONTH AVG: [paste] For each, flag: Material variance (>15% delta) POSITIVE/NEGATIVE · One-time vs recurring · Pattern continuation (3rd month?) vs one-month deviation. Explain the why

Action items
Convert variances + explanations into a short action list. Tag each: - IGNORE: known one-off - INVESTIGATE: specific thing to check - FIX: recurring issue to change - WATCH: monitor before acting Output: 3-5 item bulleted list. Skip items with no action.

Where to run these prompts.
ChatGPT, Claude, Notion AI — strengths, limits, when each wins.
Tier guide
Free + Paid
ChatGPT Free Tier
Best for: negotiation scripts, vendor messages, prompt iteration. Conversational tone, fast revisions.

Limit: ~3,000-4,000 word context per single message. If you paste a long expense list, split it: batch 1, analysis, batch 2, ask to combine.

Free as of 2026 includes GPT-4o-mini free + GPT-4o limited daily.
Claude Free (claude.ai)
Best for: P&L anomaly detection, customer profitability, anything with longer input + structured table output.

Limit: daily message limit on free tier. Wait or switch to ChatGPT.

Strength: Claude handles very long pastes — useful for cross-product workflows pasting from 3+ spreadsheets.
Notion AI
Best for: if you also own Notion Life OS. Lives inside your workspace. Most per-product reference prompts paste directly into a Notion AI block.

Cost: $10/mo add-on. Worth it if using Notion Life OS — reads your databases in-context.

Doesn't: things Claude/ChatGPT do better with full context. Use for in-context Notion tasks only.
Paid tier — worth it?
Worth it if: using these prompts heavily over 2-3 months (year-end tax prep, big finance merge, business launch period).

Paid tiers run faster, allow larger uploads (helpful for cross-product workflows), higher daily limits.

Skip if: occasional use. Free tiers do the job for individual prompts.
Universal tips
  1. Paste data as text, not screenshots. AI in free tiers can't read images well.
  2. Read the worked example first. It tells you what good output looks like.
  3. Iterate. "Make it 30 words shorter" / "be more direct" — AI revises on request.
  4. Save what works. Copy great paragraphs into your spreadsheet's Notes column. Otherwise it disappears with the conversation.
  5. Never paste account numbers, full SSN, or passwords. Use placeholders for sensitive numbers.
  6. Workflows beat standalone prompts for big decisions. The 10 cross-product workflows are the bundle's unique value.
